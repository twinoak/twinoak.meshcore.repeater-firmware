**One thing: the unit now says what *flowed*, and not only what flows.**

## A reading is a moment, and only one moment ever gets asked

A telemetry request is answered with the INA3221's last completed average, which
is a true reading of a moment. The trouble is *which* moment. A unit's own Walter
asks while it is awake with its modem up, so a week of the first unit's hourly
readings says its load drew **109 to 118 mA** with peaks of 147 to 171, and the
battery channel said the same with the sign turned. There is no reading from
while the Walter sleeps, which is nearly all of the time — and a server drawing
those moments as if they were the hour charts a unit that draws a fraction of
that.

**So the radio adds the readings up.** It never sleeps and it sees every one of
the INA3221's 845 ms averaging windows; from `v0.1.3` it accumulates charge and
energy per channel and reports the totals beside the three readings. **A reader
takes the difference between two answers and has what really flowed between
them**, whenever it happened to ask. Nine new Cayenne-LPP channels, each a
four-byte **unsigned** generic sensor (type 100) on its own channel:

| LPP channel | What | Unit |
|---:|---|---|
| 6 | panel, charge | 0.01 mAh |
| 7 | panel, energy | 0.1 mWh |
| 8 | battery, charge **in** | 0.01 mAh |
| 9 | battery, charge **out** | 0.01 mAh |
| 10 | battery, energy **in** | 0.1 mWh |
| 11 | battery, energy **out** | 0.1 mWh |
| 12 | load, charge | 0.01 mAh |
| 13 | load, energy | 0.1 mWh |
| 14 | the seconds the totals cover | 1 s |

The battery keeps its two directions apart, because a pack that charged 80 mAh
and drained 80 mAh in an hour is not a pack that did nothing. Channel 14 is what
makes the other eight readable: it is the time those totals actually cover, so a
difference of 3600 against an hour of wall clock means every window was seen,
and less means some sweeps were a gap — a channel that did not answer, the
five-second back-off after a bus failure — and says honestly how much.

**Three rules for reading them**, and the second is the one that bites:

- **A total is read as a difference, never as a value.** Two answers, channel 12
  at 84.99 mAh and then 115.22 mAh, say the load drew 30.23 mAh in that hour.
- **Channel 14 going *down* means the radio restarted** and every total began
  again from zero. The totals live in RAM; nothing writes them to flash. A
  reader that subtracts across that boundary gets a negative number, which is
  its signal to throw the interval away rather than chart it.
- **All nine arrive together or not at all.** A radio in its first averaging
  window has counted nothing and answers channels 1 to 5 alone: there is nothing
  yet to subtract, and a zero would read as a number.

Above 16 777 216 steps a total is rounded to the nearest value a `float` holds —
a multiple of 2 steps, then 4, and so on — which is still never lower than the
answer before it, so a difference is still a difference; it is simply granular.
Channel 14 crosses that line after **194 days** of uptime, the charge channels
after about 350 days at 20 mA, the energy channels after about 2.4 years. At the
top a total **saturates** at 4 294 967 040 steps and stays there; it never wraps.
That is 42 949 Ah, some 245 years of a 20 mA load, so what will be seen is the
rounding and never the ceiling.
[**docs/link.md**](https://github.com/twinoak/twinoak.meshcore.repeater-firmware/blob/v0.1.3/docs/link.md)
§2d is the table and the whole of *Reading channels 6 to 14*, and
`tools/linkcli.py lpp` prints all fourteen channels with their units.

## What the totals are not

**Not a fuel gauge.** The battery channel resolves 2 mA per step — the hardware
repository's `CURRENT-SENSE.md` §3 calls that *too coarse for coulomb counting*
and it is right. These totals are good for a day's energy and an hour's average
current; **state of charge stays a matter of voltage**. And they are not a
history: nothing is stored, so a restart is a new beginning and channel 14 is
how a reader finds out.

## Channels 1 to 5 are unchanged

Byte for byte. `v0.1.2`'s whole 36-byte telemetry answer for a given snapshot is
now the **prefix** of a 90-byte one, with the MCU's own temperature still last
and the nine totals in between — a test holds the old bytes against the new
ones, over all eight permission masks and on both the mesh path and the box
link's `LPP_GET`. Nothing moved and nothing was renumbered; this release only
adds fields. Channels 2 to 14 need the **environment** permission, as channels 2
to 5 always have; a guest at base permission still gets channel 1 and nothing
else.

**What it costs.** The Cayenne-LPP grows from 36 to 90 bytes against the mesh's
own 180-byte cap, so a telemetry reply on air goes from 54 to 102 bytes and —
at this band's SF8, 62.5 kHz, CR 4/8 — from **640 ms to 1033 ms** of airtime.
That makes it the most expensive single packet this firmware sends. Telemetry is
asked for hourly, so it is 0.03 % of the hour, and the 90 bytes still free are
there to allow another field and not to invite one.

## The reading schedule was wrong, and is now the device's own

Up to `v0.1.2` a sweep happened on a one-second timer of ours while the part was
averaging over 845 ms, so **18 % of the windows were never read at all** and the
window that was read had its average taken for a whole second. From `v0.1.3` a
sweep begins when the INA3221 raises its **conversion-ready flag**, which is the
part saying a window has finished — so the windows tile time, every microsecond
between two good sweeps belongs to exactly one interval, and the totals above
cover *all* of the time rather than 84 % of it. The configuration word is byte
for byte what it was: the part has been averaging 128 samples since the firmware
first configured it, and what was wrong was the arithmetic in a comment that
said 64.

Two things follow for anybody watching a console. The **first reading now
arrives one window (about 845 ms) after a restart** instead of immediately —
those registers held no completed average at that moment anyway. And if a part
ever did not raise the flag, the reader still reads it after two windows, keeps
answering, and counts that sweep as a **gap** rather than pretending it covered
the time.

## The Walter it goes with

**`v0.1.4`** — the Walter firmware's current release, published 10 November 2026.
**It needs no change for this release**, and it passes the telemetry on as it is:
its LPP decoder already knows the generic sensor (type 100, four bytes,
unsigned, one per step), its reply buffer is 184 bytes where this answer is 90,
and it reads up to 24 readings where this answer has 18. So the nine reach
meshmaster.dk the day a unit takes this image, with no release of the other box
in between.

It speaks **link 1.2**, which is this release's link too: nothing in the box
protocol changed here, `caps` is still `0xff`, and `POWER_GET` still carries the
three readings and no totals — the Walter decides how long to sleep from a
voltage and a current it reads while it is itself awake. Whether the box link
should carry a total or an average is a question for the specification and not
something this release answers.

## meshmaster.dk

Until the server's own pile lands, the nine show on a unit's page as *Channel 6
genericSensor*, *Channel 7 genericSensor* and so on — **nine numbers that only
grow. That is them working.** Reading a total as a difference, naming a
repeater's channels and taking battery power from the battery channel are the
server's half, and they are written from this firmware's channel table above,
which is a contract from here on.

## What it is

The same **MeshCore repeater** as `v0.1.0` through `v0.1.2`: upstream's
`simple_repeater` from the pin `repeater-v1.18.0` (`81dbae59`), compiled
unmodified and subclassed, with the link to the Walter, a clock for a board with
no RTC, the radio half of a MeshMaster exit node, and an image that says what it
is. The identity rule `v0.1.2` added is unchanged — a key is made only where no
node was, and a file system that will not mount is a **lost identity** and not a
new node ([`docs/flashing.md`](https://github.com/twinoak/twinoak.meshcore.repeater-firmware/blob/v0.1.3/docs/flashing.md)
§3a). **NoiseScope**, the spectrum and noise scanner, is in this release as
before; it compiles the same current-sense reader, so its image carries the new
reading schedule, and it speaks no link and sends no telemetry, so none of the
nine channels exists in it.

## What has been on a board, and what has not

**`v0.1.2` is on a board; this image is not.** The first unit's radio has run
`v0.1.2` since 8 November 2026. Before that it ran `v0.1.1` from 7 November,
installed over SWD by its own Walter as a first install, which is the golden
image it still has to roll back to.

So this release can reach that radio **over LTE** — *Offer a release*, a wake,
*Apply the candidate* — and for the first time both halves of that path are
really there: when `v0.1.2` was published, a Walter's download of any image
failed in two seconds, because no download had ever sent the version header
meshmaster.dk requires. The server has served headerless downloads since
8 November 2026 16:17 UTC for the units already out there, and the Walter sends
the header itself from its `v0.1.3`. The cable is still the other way, from this
release's `.uf2` after `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS`.

**Everything `v0.1.3` adds is held by host tests and by the link loop, and by no
radio.** The arithmetic is held to the millisecond — an hour at 100 mA is exactly
100 mAh and 3600 s covered; a failed channel, a back-off and a late sweep are
each a gap that moves no total; a year at full scale on every channel overflows
nothing — and the bytes of all fourteen channels are held against hand-computed
hex, with the mesh path and `LPP_GET` proved equal field for field. What no host
test can reach is the part itself: whether a real INA3221 raises that flag once
per window, and what two answers an hour apart say against a load a meter has
measured.
