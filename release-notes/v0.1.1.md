**The radio says which band it is on.** `HELLO`'s answer and `EV_BOOT` now end
with the image's `band` — `868` for this release — and the box link these images
speak is **1.1**. That is the whole of this release, and the reason is one
sentence: MeshMaster picks a repeater's image by role, board **and** band, so
until the radio said its band, a unit whose Walter carried no golden image for
it could never be offered its first image over LTE.

Nothing else of the link changed: no new request, no new event, no new
capability bit (`caps` is still `0x7f`), and no payload that existed before was
touched. The band is read out of the running image's own descriptor, like
`role`, `board` and `fw_version`, and never from a build flag — so a radio that
disagrees with the file that was flashed on it is a finding, and is meant to be.

## A 0.1.0 Walter still speaks to it

Nothing in the other box has to move first. A Walter that knows only link 1.0
asks in 1.0 and is answered in 1.0, with no band at the end — exactly what it
got from `v0.1.0`. And it can flash this release: every image entry says
`requires.link_major: 1`, which is the only version a Walter checks before it
offers an image, and the `role`, `board` and `fw_version` it holds the radio's
`HELLO` against after the flash are where they have always been. The band is
the one thing a 1.0 Walter does not get, which is the one thing it never had.

## What it is

The same **MeshCore repeater** as `v0.1.0`: upstream's `simple_repeater` from
the pin `repeater-v1.18.0` (`81dbae59`), compiled unmodified and subclassed,
with the link to the Walter, a clock for a board with no RTC, the radio half of
a MeshMaster exit node, and an image that says what it is. **NoiseScope**, the
spectrum and noise scanner, is in this release as before; it does not speak the
link, so it has no band to report, and its image carries `link 1.1` in its
descriptor only because a release's link version is one field for the whole
release.

## How to flash it

[**docs/flashing.md**](https://github.com/twinoak/twinoak.meshcore.repeater-firmware/blob/v0.1.1/docs/flashing.md)
is the one page: which file is the repeater and which is the scanner, the `.uf2`
and the `.zip` over the adapter's USB-C, the `.bin` through the Walter over SWD,
the base `.hex` for a radio fresh from the factory, what to check afterwards,
and the admin password nobody knows until somebody sets one.

**New in that page: §3a, *A radio left half-written*.** On 6 November 2026 a
Walter at `v0.1.0` began a first install over SWD and its own watchdog reset it
part-way through. The radio came back over its own USB-C, from `v0.1.0`'s own
`.uf2`, **with its key** — because the file system that holds a node's identity,
its settings, its ACL and its clock is above the application region and neither
the Walter's flasher nor a `.uf2` may write there. §3a is what to do, and why it
is safe.
[docs/provisioning.md](https://github.com/twinoak/twinoak.meshcore.repeater-firmware/blob/v0.1.1/docs/provisioning.md)
is still what makes a board *this* unit.

## What has been on a board, and what has not

**Not this image.** What a board *has* shown, on 6 November 2026, is the one
thing above: the first unit's radio took a half-written application over SWD and
came back intact from `v0.1.0`'s published `.uf2`, with its identity. The band
in `HELLO` has been held by the host tests against both fixture files of the
specification — every case of link 1.0 and of 1.1 — and by the link loop end to
end, and not yet by a radio and a dongle:
[docs/bench.md](https://github.com/twinoak/twinoak.meshcore.repeater-firmware/blob/v0.1.1/docs/bench.md)
§2 is the three lines that would.

## The Walter it goes with

**None yet.** The Walter firmware's first release is `v0.1.0` of its own
repository and it knows link 1.0, so it reports no band; the build that reads
one is that repository's pile 8, unreleased. This release is what has to be on
the radio first, which is why it is here before that one.
