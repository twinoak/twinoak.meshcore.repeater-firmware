## Decisions taken without asking

1. **The notes lead with the correction, not with the feature.** The task lists
   01 first and 02 second, and that is the order kept: a person reading a release
   body for a radio in the field needs to know that the last release's notes were
   wrong about the one thing that cost an identity before they need to know what
   a Walter can now read. The two sections are one sentence each in the task's
   terms and a paragraph each in the body, because both facts have a *why* a
   reader cannot reconstruct.
2. **The `IDENTITY LOST` line is quoted verbatim in the notes.** It is the one
   string a person will meet at a console and search for, and a release body that
   paraphrased it would be a body that does not match what the radio says.
3. **The Walter paragraph says what a `v0.1.1` Walter cannot tell you**, in one
   short paragraph. The task asks only for the Walter's current release and that
   a Walter before `v0.1.2` speaks 1.1 and does not ask for these; but a reader
   of these notes may well be the person who meets a radio in the lost mode
   through a `v0.1.1` Walter, and the honest sentence is cheap. It is checked
   against that firmware's code, and it corrects 01's follow-up where that was
   imprecise (above, and in the follow-up below).
4. **README gains a bullet for the identity rule.** *What the firmware does
   today* had nothing about how a key comes to be, and 01's change is the kind of
   thing that belongs in that list rather than only in a page about flashing.
5. **No *Latest* claim is made before the release exists.** The notes file names
   `blob/v0.1.2/...` links, which is what every previous release's notes do and
   which resolve the moment the tag exists.
