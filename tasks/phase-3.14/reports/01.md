# p3.14-01 — A person may ask again

Reason 4 — *a person asked for the Walter* — is no longer held to the hour the other five reasons
are held to. A new one is raised when **300 s** have passed since the last one was raised, while
the battery is at its normal level; while it is low, critical or **not known** it keeps the hour.
Either limit is counted from when the last one was raised, by the limit that holds when the new
one is asked for (`spec/LINK-PROTOCOL.md` §8's last paragraph, §10's row *A new attention of the
same reason*).

The limit now travels **with the raise**: `AttentionMachine::raise` takes a fourth argument,
`limit_ms`, whose default is the hour — so reasons 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 are what they were, to the
line — and `src/roles/repeater/link/BoardLink.cpp`'s `raiseWalterWake()` is the one place that
passes anything else. Which of the two it passes is decided there, because the machine has no
battery and the board has one. Nothing on the wire changed, the link's version did not move, and
no byte of `spec/` or of the pinned MeshCore was touched.

## What the task asked to be said in the report

**What the watch answers before its first reading, and on a board whose sensor does not answer.**
`BatteryWatch::level()` answers **`kBatteryNormal`** in both cases, and that is why the question
reason 4 asks is not *what is the level* but *is the level known and normal*:

- **Before its first reading** the watch is in `kBatteryNormal` because that is the level it is
  constructed in. `lastMilliVolts()` is 0 and `pendingLevel()` is `kBatteryNoChange`; nothing has
  told it anything. On this board the first valid reading of channel 2 is about 845 ms after
  `begin()` — one averaging window of the INA3221's own (`PowerReader.h`).
- **On a board whose sensor does not answer** — no INA3221, the always-on 3.3 V rail down, a
  device in its five-second back-off — `poll(false, …)` *holds the level where it is* on purpose
  ("no reading is not a reading of zero volts"), so the answer stays whatever it last was, which
  on such a board is `kBatteryNormal` for ever.

Both therefore keep the hour, because *known* is the reading's validity bit and not the level:
`levelKnownNormal(level, reading_valid)` is `reading_valid && level == kBatteryNormal`, and
`power::batteryKnownNormal()` feeds it `g_power.readings().batteryValid()`. **Nothing of the
watch's behaviour changed** — not a threshold, not the 100 mV hysteresis, not the minute below.

**Where the limit or the refusal count is printed.** Nowhere, and that is the whole list:

- `rateLimited()` and `raised()` are read by **no** caller outside the tests
  (`grep -rn 'rateLimited\|\.raised()' src/ tools/` finds only the accessor's own line in
  `Attention.h`): they are
  not in `STATUS`, not in `COUNTERS`, not in a setting and not on the console. `STATUS`'s
  `attention` field and `sensor get walter.wake` both answer `pendingReason()`, which is unchanged
  — so a request the limit refused still reads `1` while an earlier one is pending and `0`
  otherwise (this phase's third follow-up is about exactly that, and is not this task's).
- `sensor set walter.wake 1` answers `ok` as before, whatever the machine did with it
  (`Settings::set` is untouched), and `sensor get walter.wake` reads what it read.

So what had to follow the rule was **words**, and these are all of them:
`src/common/link/attention/Attention.h` (the file's own description, the two constants, `raise()`,
`rateLimited()`, the limit's arrays), `Attention.cpp`'s age check,
`src/common/link/session/Interfaces.h`, `src/common/board/RoleHooks.h`,
`src/common/power/BatteryWatch.h`, `src/common/power/BoardPower.h`,
`src/roles/repeater/link/BoardLink.h`, `src/roles/repeater/app/Settings.h` (both places the task
names, ~L43 and ~L100), `tools/linkloop/linkloop.cpp`'s header,
`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §4 and §6, `docs/link.md` §2c and its table of where things live, and
`docs/bench.md` §4. `release-notes/unreleased/p3.14-01.md` is the one line a person reads.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The task says both objects live in `BoardLink.cpp`; the battery does not.** The attention
  machine is there (`g_attention`), but `BatteryWatch` and the INA3221 reader live in
  `src/common/power/BoardPower.cpp`, which is the one file of `src/common/power/` that knows what
  a RAK4630 is and is shared with the scanner role. The decision is still taken where the task
  says — `BoardLink.cpp`'s `raiseWalterWake()` is the line that chooses the limit — through one
  new accessor, `power::batteryKnownNormal()`, declared in `BoardPower.h` beside
  `batteryMilliVolts()`. Nothing else was moved.
- **No disagreement between `spec/` and the task** was found. §8's new paragraph and §10's row say
  what the task says: 300 s, the normal level *above the low threshold with its hysteresis*, the
  hour for low, critical and not known, and either limit counted from when the last one was raised
  by the limit that holds when the new one is asked for. `spec/` was read in this working tree and
  is byte for byte its manifest's.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The limit is a fourth argument of `raise()`, defaulting to the hour**, rather than a table of
  limits inside the machine or a `bool battery_normal` the machine interprets. The machine then
  stays what its header says it is — portable, no clock, no board, no battery — and the one reason
  that has two limits is told which by the caller that knows. The default is what keeps the other
  five reasons, and every existing call of `raise()`, exactly as they were.
- **`levelKnownNormal(level, reading_valid)` is an inline function in `BatteryWatch.h`**, not a
  method and not a line of board code. The alternative was that this one rule — *not known is not
  normal* — lived only in `BoardPower.cpp`, which no native test can compile (it includes
  upstream's headers and Arduino's), so it would have been the only part of the task with no test.
  As a pure function of the two facts it is tested in `test_power` against a real `BatteryWatch`,
  and the watch's class, its thresholds, its hysteresis and its minimum time below are untouched —
  the file's diff is that function and three sentences of comment.
- **"Not known" is read as "channel 2's last reading was not valid."** That covers the two cases
  the task names (before the first reading, and a sensor that does not answer) and one it does
  not: a sensor that answered and then stopped, including the five-second back-off a slow or
  silent device is put in. All three keep the hour, which is the cautious way round — the pack is
  what the unit lives on, and a level nobody has confirmed is not a licence to spend an LTE attach
  every five minutes.
- **`tools/linkloop/` mirrors the board's line.** It holds a real `AttentionMachine`, a real
  `BatteryWatch` and a real `PowerReader` over a fake INA3221, and it is the one place this rule
  can be watched with no box at all, so its `walter.wake` host method now asks the same question
  and passes the same limit (`LoopSettingsHost::raiseWalterWake`, out of line because the watch is
  declared below the class). `--wake` goes through that same method instead of raising reason 4 by
  itself, so there is one road and not two; at startup, before any reading, the level is not known
  and the limit is the hour. `out/verify/linkloop.log` still shows `EV_ATTENTION reason 4`,
  `repeat 0..3 of 10` after a `sleep 600`, which is that road exercised end to end.
- **The three bench lines went into `docs/bench.md` §4** (*Management over the link*), beside the
  `walter.wake` lines phase 1's task 05 wrote, and not into a new section: they are the same
  person at the same port with the same Walter. §4 is not one of the six walked sections
  (`docs/bench.md` §0b), so `tools/bench/walk.py` has no line to add — `python tools/bench/bench.py
  walk 4` refuses by name, and `test_walk.py`'s page tests hold only §1, §3, §5, §8, §9 and §10.
- **Phase 1's own bench line was reworded**, because it said something that is no longer true: *A
  second `walter.wake` inside the hour raises nothing … until an hour has passed*. It is now *A
  second `walter.wake` straight away raises nothing*, with the limit named and the three new lines
  pointed at. It was not ticked, so nothing anybody had confirmed was changed.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session opens a port for this task (the phase's README: *Not on the board*), and these lines
need a **Walter or `linkcli.py` on the link, a pack on the bench and two six-minute waits** — and
§4 is not a walked section, so `bench walk` cannot run them even though `bench.local.json` is on
this machine. They are in `docs/bench.md` §4 in the order a person would do them, with the
numbers to write in:

1. **Two requests six minutes apart wake it twice.** `linkcli.py sleep 600`,
   `cli "sensor set walter.wake 1"`, `listen 4` shows the two `0x00` bytes and `EV_ATTENTION`
   reason 4; a `status` ends it (`attention 4` in that answer, `0` in the next). Six minutes later
   the same two lines raise a **second** sequence, `repeat` counting from 0 again. This is the
   whole point of the task.
2. **Two requests two minutes apart wake it once.** The same with two minutes between them: the
   second `sensor set walter.wake 1` answers `ok`, `listen 10` is silent, `status` says
   `attention 0`. Two minutes is where meshmaster.dk's second wake line falls when its reply was
   lost, which is why the limit is 300 s and not nothing.
3. **A pack the firmware reads as low keeps the hour.** `power.low_mv` set just above what the
   pack reads, then the minute the threshold needs (`EV_ATTENTION` reason 2 is the sign it took);
   two requests six minutes apart now wake it **once**. `power.low_mv` to be put back afterwards,
   which also leaves §5's two lines as they were found.
4. **The reworded phase 1 line** (a second `walter.wake` straight away raises nothing) — it was
   never ticked, and its new wording is walked with 1 and 2.

What *was* verified without a board: the machine and the watch natively (522 tests), the whole
rule through the firmware's own session as a host program (`tools/linkloop/`, driven by
`tools/linkcli.py` through a pipe), and both board images built with `-Werror` on our sources.
