# p3.16-04 — The garden is the bench

`hunt.py` and `echoes.py` were built for a board on USB with a steady carrier. The echoes are not
there. They have only ever been seen on the unit at Bellevue, so they are measured in a NoiseScope
session started from meshmaster.dk — six typed steps of at most forty characters, one file of lines
an hour later, and a signal that is a neighbour's traffic and so is in some frames and not in
others.

This task makes the two tools work that way. `hunt.py --sessions` turns a list into session
programs and **checks every rule the two repositories that take them have** before anybody types
one; the four lists of the pile's README are files; `echoes.py --steps` reads a download as one
table across its steps and then **every two of its scopes that differ in exactly one thing**, with
the verdict the hunt is after. **Tools and words only: nothing under `src/` changed, and there is
no release note** — a unit runs exactly what it ran before this branch.

**There was no board and none was wanted** (the pile's README: *03 and 04 need no board*), and no
session had been run, so every verdict is *Not verified here*. What is verified here is that the
four programs would be taken, to the byte, and that each rule that would refuse one refuses it by
name.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

1. **Session A's steps are 85 seconds and not the README's 90**, for the arithmetic above. The
   README's own 90 is in the tests, as the number that is refused.
2. **`scope_plans.json` gained four rows**, where 03 deliberately left it unedited. Its reason does
   not apply here: 03 added *words*, which are not arithmetic, and this task adds **spans** — the
   garden types whole megahertz (`scope 780 960 0 467`, not `scope 779.618 959.618 0 467`) because
   forty characters leave no room for the decimals, and those are plans no row held. The four are
   the wide span, the same span 7 MHz lower, the 60 MHz of sessions C and D, and the 13 MHz narrow
   step. They were computed with the Python copy and are **confirmed by the firmware's own**
   `Scope.ThePlanTableIsPlanScopes`, which passed in the native run — which is the whole point of
   that file.
3. **The server's two numbers cannot be reached before the Walter's, and are checked anyway.** Six
   steps of 65 536 bytes is 393 216, under the 512 kB the server keeps; six steps of as many of the
   shortest frames (270 bytes at sixteen points) as 65 536 bytes hold is 1 452 lines, under 4 000.
   So no list can break either, and no test breaks either — instead
   `test_the_servers_two_numbers_cannot_be_reached_before_the_walters` holds **the implication**,
   so a Walter that one day keeps more of a step fails there and says why the two checks exist.
4. **Two test helpers changed; no test method did.** `plan_of()` gained `seg_mhz` and `settle_us`
   as defaulted keywords (a spec written before 02 is a spec still), and `with_new_members()` now
   calls a new `grow_frame()` so that one function grows one frame for both it and the garden's
   captures — its behaviour is unchanged and
   `test_a_capture_with_the_new_members_reads_exactly_as_one_without` still passes untouched.
   `test_hunt.py`'s module docstring gained a third bullet.
5. **No new bullet in `docs/bench.md` §9**, for the reason 02 and 03 both gave:
   `tools/bench/walk.py` holds that section's bullets one for one against its walk, so a new
   bullet is a new walk step and this session has no board to write one against. The image check's
   existing bullet names `p3.16-04` and the two commands that read a session instead.
6. **Five pair kinds and not six.** The task names *the first point*, *the regulator*, *the gain*,
   *the width* and *the settle*; a **bandwidth** pair is not formed, because the task names the
   bandwidth only as a thing a first-point pair must agree on — and in truth two scopes of one span
   at two bandwidths differ in the step and the point count as well, so they are never one thing
   apart. The four garden lists contain no such pair.
7. **A step's bytes are the bound and not what a line really measures.** `scopeFrameBytes()` says
   762 at 386 points and a real line was 705 (03's report). The bound is what the firmware itself
   sizes its sink with, and a refusal that is eight per cent cautious is the right side of this
   one: a step that overruns is cut off `full`, and a session that has to be run again costs
   Bellevue the whole half hour. It is also what makes 90 seconds refused and 85 taken; with the
   705 both would pass. `test_a_frame_is_never_longer_than_the_bytes_a_step_is_priced_with` holds
   the bound over both real lines of 03's report and over every `.ndjson` under
   `tools/noisescope/captures/` — of which there are none today, which is the same sentence as
   *Captures read*.
8. **`--steps` says when a step carries no `ack`.** Not asked for, and the one way this table could
   be quietly wrong: the width, the settle and the two words are the ack's and are written nowhere
   else, and two steps of **one** span — garden-b's first three — are two scopes only because their
   acks differ. A download carries every line the scanner sent, acks included; a file that lost
   them now says so per step rather than reading three steps as one in silence
   (`test_a_step_that_lost_its_ack_is_said_to_have_lost_it`).

## Decisions taken without asking

1. **A step brings `ceil(seconds × 1000 / interval)` frames**, where the interval is the one the
   line asked for or the port's floor, whichever is longer — because that is the one the `ack` says
   it took, and a session's lines come out on the Walter's 115200 baud UART. Frames at 0, one
   interval, two intervals… inside the step's own seconds: 85 at a second over 85 seconds. The
   newline is in the bytes already (`scopeFrameBytes()` counts it).
2. **One frame-size rule for all four lists**, the current one, rather than one rule for
   session A's 0.1.7 scanner and another for the rest. A tool that prices a line differently
   depending on which image will run it is a tool nobody can check.
3. **Eighteen minutes for the two flashes and the proof.** The README says about 27 minutes for a
   program of nine minutes of steps; 27 − 9 is what is left, and it is named once
   (`SESSION_OFF_MESH_OVERHEAD_S`) with the README beside it.
4. **"No word" is a value of its own when two scopes are paired.** A step with no `reg` and a step
   with `dcdc` are a pair that differs in the regulator, not one scope read twice — even though
   today they are the same chip setting. What the table reports is what the session **asked**, and
   `-` is printed for a word that was not on the line, which `ack_gain()`'s docstring says is not
   `boost`: the gain such a scope ran at is whatever `boost on|off` left and no capture can
   recover it.
5. **The comment line directly over a step is what that step asks**, and the one over a `---` is
   what the session asks. One line, not a paragraph: the reader accumulates nothing, so a
   two-line comment would print only its second line. Said in `read_sessions()`' docstring, and
   the four lists keep theirs to one line.
6. **The quiet rule is `--steps`' and nothing else's.** `ScopeReading` takes it as
   `quiet_rule=False`, so every other caller — `echoes.py` without `--steps`, `hunt.py`'s bench
   table, `run.json` — reads exactly what it read before. And `--floor`, said outright, keeps the
   floor whatever the frames are: a person who names a floor means it.
7. **A place's height in the quiet frames is read entirely in the quiet frames** — the quiet
   median at the place against the tenth percentile of the quiet picture in its segment. That is
   what the README's *4 to 8 dB over the floor in the quiet frames* is a measurement of; mixing
   the lit frames' floor into it would measure something else.
8. **Only a `scope` step is sized.** Another verb's lines — a `stat`, a `hist`, a `dwell` — are
   `-` in the table and are not refused by the Walter's rule, because this tool cannot say how
   many lines they bring. Every step of the four lists is a `scope`.
9. **The first-point verdict counts the places that match on one criterion only.** A place that
   matches on both (which happens where the shift and the interval conspire) counts for neither
   side, a tie is *cannot say*, and the two shapes the task names outright — a shift that is a
   whole multiple of the interval, and fewer than three echoes in either scope — are checked
   before the counts and are said in words, with the multiple or the counts.
10. **A pair must agree on the point count as well as the step and the bandwidth.** The task's
    list for a first-point pair is step, bandwidth, width, settle, regulator and gain; a span
    shifted with the same step keeps its point count anyway, so this costs nothing and it stops
    two scopes of the same step and different lengths being compared place for place.
11. **A `dev` bit is named only where this repository names it** — the three constants of
    `src/roles/scanner/Sweep.h` and the image calibration's, which `docs/noisescope.md` §5 reads
    off the data sheet. Any other bit prints as *bit N, which no file here names* rather than
    being given a meaning from memory.
12. **Each list marks its one session with `--- <name>`** even though a file with no mark is one
    session named after the file (which is tested). It costs a line and it makes the printed name
    and the format the lists are read with explicit.
13. **A step's seconds come from the list and never from the command.** `seconds = N` before the
    steps it applies to, 85 when it is never said.

## Not verified here (and why)

**Every verdict, and that is the design.** There was no board in this session and no session had
been run on the unit. What follows is what the garden has to show, one line per session, and the
sessions are the pile's README's word for word.

* **Session A — `tools/noisescope/lists/garden-a.txt`, and it needs nothing new.** The scanner of
  0.1.7 that is on the unit runs every line of it. Paste the six lines above into the form, save
  *Download the lines* into `tools/noisescope/captures/garden/`, and
  `python tools/noisescope/echoes.py --steps <file> --carrier 869.618`. **The pair of steps 1 and
  2 is the answer**: *moves with the segments* means the echoes are the sweep's own and a segment
  width is the mend; *stays with the frequency* means they belong to the signal or the site and
  the DC-DC converter at about 5 MHz is the reading to chase; *cannot say* means the counts did
  not separate and step 3 (2.8 MHz lower) is the second try. Steps 5 and 6 ask it again at an
  eighth of the bandwidth, where an echo of the segment sits at 873.2 and then 876.2 MHz and one
  of the signal near 874.4 in both.
* **Session B — `garden-b.txt`**, after a release carrying 02 and 03 and *Fetch a scanner image*
  on the unit. The regulator pair (steps 1 and 2) and the gain pair (steps 1 and 3) are printed as
  counts and decibels and nothing more; step 6's `us` is the one that settles
  `kScopeUpdateMsKhz` — **about 83 000 if an RSSI update is 1.6 ms / RBW and about 48 000 if it is
  Semtech's 0.94** — and `dev` of the three wide steps should read `0000` throughout.
* **Session C — `garden-c.txt`**: four widths of the 60 MHz around the signal, as three width
  pairs plus the narrow one. **Lower nothing on the strength of one session**: which width `scope`
  takes when none is given is the task after this pile, from the tables.
* **Session D — `garden-d.txt`**: four settles, as three settle pairs plus the narrow one. This is
  the list with nothing to spare — 384 bytes of 384 — so it cannot gain a character.

**And four things about the tools themselves that only a real download can settle:**

* **Whether a session's download really carries each step's `ack`.** Everything the table says
  about a width, a settle, a regulator or a gain comes from there, and so does telling garden-b's
  first three steps apart — they are one span asked three ways. *Download the lines* is documented
  as *every line as the scanner sent it*, which includes the ack, and `--steps` now says per step
  when one is missing (*Deviations* 8). The first download settles it; if the acks are not there,
  that is a finding about meshmaster.dk's download and not about this tool.
* **Whether a frame is really 762 bytes and not 705.** Every `bytes back` figure above is the
  bound. The first session says which: 85 frames of a wide step either come back whole or the step
  ends `full`, and the download's own byte count against 64 770 is the measurement.
* **Whether 85 seconds really brings 85 frames.** `ceil(seconds × 1000 / interval)` is arithmetic,
  not a measurement: when the Walter starts counting, and whether a frame landing on the step's
  last millisecond is kept, are the unit's and the Walter's. A download of 84 or 86 frames is the
  honest answer and the number to put in `step_lines()`.
* **Whether the quiet-frame rule finds anything in a real garden capture.** It is held here to
  planted frames, where a place stands 24 dB over the floor when the signal is there and 5 dB when
  it is not. Session 3's own frames were read by hand as 14 to 35 dB and 4 to 8 dB; whether
  `--over 6` separates those in a real file, and whether ten lit frames of sixty give a median
  worth anything, is what the first download shows. `--lit` and `--over` are there to move.
