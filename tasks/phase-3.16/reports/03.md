# p3.16-03 — a scope that accounts for itself

Two readings of the echoes are open and one number this firmware is built on is in doubt. This
task does not decide any of them — it makes all three **askable from a NoiseScope session**, which
is where they will be decided, because the echoes have only ever been seen on the unit at
Bellevue and a session's step is one typed line of at most forty characters.

So: `scope` takes four **words** — `ldo`, `dcdc`, `boost`, `noboost` — anywhere after the two
ends, each holding for that one scope; every frame says `us` and `usx`, how long the plugin's own
calls took for the shortest and the longest whole sweep in it; and every frame of a plan with
more than one segment says `dev`, the OR of the chip's `GetDeviceErrors` word read where the arm
already stands in standby. **A `scope` asked for as today measures as today**: the plan, the
`ack` and the arm's calls are `main`'s to the byte, bar the one read and one clear at each arm of
a multi-segment plan, and the tests that held them before this branch pass with their assertions
untouched.

**There was no board and none was needed** (the pile's README: *03 and 04 need no board*). The
proof of all of it is the first NoiseScope session on the unit, and *Not verified here* says what
that session has to show, one line per deliverable.

**The new length, and the port's rule.** The three members are at most 46 bytes of envelope:
`,"us":` and ten digits (16), `,"usx":` and ten (17), `,"dev":"ffff"` (13). So `kEnvelope` is 246
and:

| points | bound on `main` | bound now | emitted, really | port's floor at 115200 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 250 | 536 | **582** | 513 (one segment, no `dev`) | 94 → **102 ms** |
| 386 | 716 | **762** | 705 | 126 → **134 ms** |
| 2048 | 2932 | **2978** | — | 510 → **518 ms** |

The floor is `ceil(bytes × 10 × 1000 / baud) × kScopeSinkDutyDivisor`, so a frame still takes at
most **half** the port's time, which is the rule it exists for — held for 16, 250, 386 and 2048
points by `ThePortsFloorIsWorkedOutFromTheFramesNewLength`. **The lists of the garden are sized
from the 762** (the Walter keeps 65 536 bytes a step): at 85 s a step that is 85 frames at a
one-second interval and about 65 kB, which is the whole budget — so a garden step either keeps the
interval at a second or takes fewer frames. That is task 04's to settle; the number it needs is
**762 bytes a frame at 386 points**.

## What the read and the clear cost, and why they are not in the plan's time

Deliverable 4 asks for the cost *by the code*, and for it to go into the plan's time *if it is more
than a rounding*. By the code, at the pin:

* `GetDeviceErrors` through `Module::SPIreadStream` is a **four-byte** stream — the opcode, the
  status byte RadioLib adds for a read (`spiConfig.widths[STATUS]` is 8) and two data bytes;
* `ClearDeviceErrors` through `SPIwriteStream` is a **three-byte** one (the opcode and two data);
* at RadioLib's default for this platform, **2 MHz** (`BuildOpt.h`, `RADIOLIB_DEFAULT_SPI_SETTINGS`;
  nothing in `platformio.ini` or `config.h` overrides it), seven bytes is **28 µs** of clocked
  bits;
* plus the two one-microsecond BUSY settles `SPItransferStream` makes after a transfer, and the
  two transactions' own overhead (two `new`/`delete` pairs, two chip-select writes, two
  `beginTransaction`/`endTransaction`). The BUSY *waits* are not a cost here: the chip is in
  standby, so each read of the pin returns at once. **About 30 µs, and under 50.**
* Neither is paid at all by a plan of one segment, which makes neither call.

**It is deliberately not in `scopeSweepMs()`.** That figure is `points` RSSI updates plus
`kScopeSegmentUpdates` (eight updates) an arm plus the settles — and the eight updates are
**27 µs at 467 kHz**, while the four SPI calls an arm already makes (`standby`, `SetRfFrequency`,
`CalibrateImage`, `setSweep`) cost several hundred microseconds that are **not** in the figure and
are exactly what p3.16-02's report says the bench has to price (its deviation 6). Adding 30 µs of
SPI to a number that already omits an order of magnitude more of it would make the number less
coherent and no truer. And as a rounding: the figure is quoted in whole milliseconds, and the
widest plan in this pile — 33 segments — pays **0.99 ms**, inside one unit of it. The `ack`'s
`sweep` and the port's interval are therefore what they were, which is what criterion 1 asks.

**Where it does show, and should:** in `sps`, which is measured and not planned. A plan of 2048
segments would pay 61 ms of it, and the arm's own unpriced SPI far more; `sps` against today's
line is where any of that is visible, which is what the bench's and the garden's tables are for.

## Decisions taken without asking

1. **One word of each pair at most — `ldo ldo` is refused too, not only `ldo dcdc`.** The task
   asks that *both words of a pair* be refused. Refusing any second word of the same pair is one
   rule instead of two, it covers the task's case, and the same word twice on a line a person is
   fitting into forty characters is a typo worth hearing about rather than a request worth
   guessing at. `scope 780 960 ldo boost` — one of each — is of course a line.
2. **Each word is named in the `ack` only when its own word was given**, so `reg=ldo` can stand
   without `gain=`. That is what the task's deliverable 2 writes, and it is the opposite of task
   02's rule for the two numbers (which are named together or not at all, because either one
   being non-default makes both worth saying). The two rules sit side by side in one function and
   the test holds all four shapes.
3. **The chip half of *the radio is what it was* is held by reading `Radio.cpp`.** A mode's
   bring-up is not host-portable, so the decision was made one pure function
   (`scopeRadioSetup()`, host-tested over every combination) and the claim that the chip half asks
   for exactly that is a grep of the file, as `test/test_rails` greps the sources its rules are
   about: `beginPlugin()` is the one place a scope's words reach the chip, `modeRadioSetup()` the
   one place every other mode's come from, `applyRxPath()` takes the gain it is to apply rather
   than reading a global again, and `stop()` drops the plan. **There is no sticky state to put
   back**, which is the strongest form of the property and the reason it can be checked this way.
4. **The error word is read and cleared only for a plan of more than one segment.** The task says
   a plan of one segment is not given a standby it does not have and that its frames carry no
   `dev`; making the read conditional on `plan.segments > 1` is the one change that gives both,
   and it also means such a plan pays nothing for a number it does not report.
5. **`out.before_errors` is OR-ed, not assigned**, so a reload inside one arm does not lose the
   word the arm read before it. A frame's `dev` is then the OR over its arms, and the arm's
   *other* read — the calibration's own verdict, `out.device_errors` — is untouched by it.
6. **A frame's `dev` is the OR of the words read at its arms**, which means the word read at a
   sweep's **first** arm belongs to the last sweep of the frame before it. The task writes both
   halves of that (*what is read at the arm of segment k is what the sweep of the segment before
   it left*, and *a frame's `dev` covers its sweeps*), and at a frame boundary they cannot both be
   literally true. The mechanism is the one the task specifies; `docs/noisescope.md` says the
   frame carries what its own sweeps left, which is true of every arm but the first of a frame,
   and `dev` is a set of sticky fault bits rather than a per-sweep measurement — a bit attributed
   one frame late is still the same answer to *did this ever happen*.
7. **`dev` is four lowercase hex digits in a JSON string.** `0010` is not a JSON number and `16`
   would lose the shape the data sheet's table has; lowercase matches the `err` line's existing
   `device errors 0x%04x`.
8. **`us` and `usx` are on every frame, `dev` only on the ones that have it.** A frame is only
   emitted after a whole sweep, so `us` and `usx` always have at least one sweep to report; an
   absent `dev` is the one and only signal that the plan had a single segment, and
   `docs/noisescope.md` says to ask for the span in two segments when the word is wanted.
9. **A plugin call that refused measures nothing.** `sweepScopeSegment()` adds no time when the
   call returned a code: the mode ends there, and a frame's `us` must not be the duration of a
   failure.
10. **`kMaxTokens` is eleven**, which is `scope` with every number and both words. The one line
    that needs all eleven is 48 characters and so cannot be typed in a session's step; it is in
    the tests as the parser's bound, and the test says so.
11. **The two words change nothing of a plan, so `scope_plans.json` gained no row.** The shared
    table exists to hold `echoes.py`'s copy of the plan arithmetic to the firmware's, and the
    words are not arithmetic; a test asserts the equality that makes the table's silence correct
    rather than leaving it to be assumed.
12. **`boost on|off` typed during a scope still reaches the chip**, as on `main`. It sets the
    setting and the chip both; a scope that asked for a gain of its own gets that gain back at its
    next reload, and the setting is what the mode after it takes. Nothing about the words makes
    the operator's own word about the gain stop working.

* **Deliverable 4, `dev`.** Every frame of the three wide-span steps has more than one segment and

  the first time this chip has said its synthesizer did not lock, and it would be the answer to
  what the retunes of the plugin cost. The narrow steps carry no `dev`, which is itself the thing
  to check: no `dev` key at all on a one-segment frame, and the span asked for as
  `85 scope 863 876 250 58.6 1000 max 7` carries one.

**Also not verified here, and each a thing only the part can settle:**

* **what the chip actually does on the LDO.** The word is passed to the vendored
  `begin(freq, tcxo, use_ldo, boost)`, which is `SX1262::beginFSK(…, useRegulatorLDO)`, which ends
  in RadioLib's `setRegulatorLDO()` (7.8.0, `SX126x.cpp:1462`). That the receiver then *works* on
  the linear regulator, at this current limit and this gain, is a claim about the part. If it does
  not, the step's `ack` is still green and its frames are floor — which the capture shows at once.
* **whether the lower gain changes the floor by what the data sheet implies.** `noboost` is the
  same call `boost off` makes, so this is not new code; what is new is that it is per-scope, and a
  session that sees *no change at all* between the `boost` and `noboost` steps would be saying the
  word did not reach the chip.
* **what the read and the clear really cost.** 30 µs is by the code (above). `sps` of a wide step
  against the same step on the release before this one is the measurement, and it is about one part
  in two hundred of a sweep — which is to say it is not expected to be visible, and that is the
  claim.
* **that `dev` is read where the word answers.** `readDeviceErrors()` is called immediately after
  `standby()` returned 0, which is RadioLib's own condition for the word being anything but zero
  (the comment in `SX126x::calibrateImage`). A board that reported `0000` for every frame
  *including* a frame whose arm failed would be saying this read is in the wrong place — and
  `tasks/phase-3.5/reports/02.md` is the session that showed it is not.
* **the board was not flashed and `info` was not read**, so no scanner image carrying the four
  words has been on a chip. Both board environments build and every test passes; that is a claim
  about the build, not about a part.

Two things this branch found that a later reader may want, neither in scope here:

* **The garden's budget is 762 bytes a frame at 386 points.** The Walter keeps 65 536 bytes a
  step, so an 85-second step of the wide span can carry about 85 frames and no more — the lists of
  task 04 are sized from that number, and a step that asks for `interval 0` on a span this wide
  will be cut off by the Walter rather than by the scanner.
* **`us` and `usx` make the arm's own cost measurable for the first time.** `sweep_ms` of the
  `ack` is planned and `sps` is measured; `us` is the plugin's share of a sweep alone, so
  `1 000 000 / us` is the sweeps a second the plugin's calls would allow and the gap between that
  and `sps / 10` is everything the plan does not price — the arms, the settles, the calibrations,
  the frame on the wire. Nothing in this branch computes it; `echoes.py --steps` could, and that
  is task 04's table to decide.
