### §3a, sentence by sentence, against the code

The task asks for three sentences and says to check each one before writing it.
Two hold as written; one does not, and the page says the true thing instead.

**1. *double-tap reset; if the application is gone, the bootloader may already be
up*.** Holds — and more strongly than *may*. The Walter's flasher marks the
bootloader's settings page *bank 0 holds nothing* **before** its first erase and
*valid* only after the whole image has been read back and its SHA-256 checked
(`flasher.cpp`, `Step::kSettingsInvalidate` … `kSettingsValidate` at the Walter's
tag `v0.1.0`), and the vector-table page is the first it erases
(`programPages`, step 4: *from here on the radio has no startable application
until the last write*). `bootloader_app_is_valid()` refuses both of those states
— first two words blank, and `bank_0 != BANK_VALID_APP` — and `check_dfu_mode()`
enters DFU on `dfu_start || !valid_app` with no timeout, so the drive appears and
stays. The double tap is independent of all of it: two **pin** resets within
`DFU_DBL_RESET_DELAY` 500 ms, decided in RAM at `0x20007F7C`. So §3a says the
drive is *very likely already there*, and the double tap is the **third** step —
with a second step the task's sketch does not have: an interrupted flash can
have left the core **halted** (`Flasher::flash` halts it and only `finish()`
resets it), so *reset the radio once* comes before pressing anything twice.

**2. *copy the release's `.uf2` onto the drive*.** Holds; §2's ordinary path, and
§3a points at it rather than repeating it.

**3. *neither the Walter's flasher nor the `.uf2` writes outside the application
region*.** **Not true of the flasher — and the untrue half is what makes the
recovery work.** The flasher writes one page outside the region on purpose: the
bootloader's settings page at `0xFF000` (`board_profile.h`: *the one page outside
`app` the flasher touches*; `flasher.cpp`: *the one function that writes outside
the application region*). What is true is the thing that matters: neither path
may write where the node's identity, its settings, its ACL and its clock live,
`0xED000`–`0xF4000`. §3a therefore names the application region
(`0x26000`–`0xED000`) **and** that one page — *which is what makes an interrupted
install announce itself* — and then the file system, which neither path may
touch.

The `.uf2` half of sentence 3 was checked twice over:

- **the file.** Every block of both images is inside the application region
  (`out/p3.8-02/uf2-blocks.txt`): the repeater 1 631 blocks `0x26000`..`0x8BF00`,
  the scanner 493 blocks `0x26000`..`0x44D00`, family `0xADA52840`, each block's
  three magic words checked.
- **the bootloader that will read it.** `write_block()` of the **vendored 0.4.4
  binary**, disassembled at `0xF8A62` (`out/p3.8-02/write_block-0.4.4.dis.txt`,
  cut from p3.4-01's objdump of the bootloader region — MIT code, never the
  SoftDevice): the three family ids it compares are `0xADA52840`, `0xD663823C`
  and `0x239A0029`; ours takes the application branch, whose whole guard is
  `sub r3, r0, #0x1000` / `cmp.w r3, #0xE9000` / `bcs` — that is
  `0x1000 <= targetAddr < 0xEA000`, and a block at or above it is refused. So a
  `.uf2` **cannot** reach the file system at `0xED000`, the bootloader at
  `0xF4000` or its settings page, whatever it carries. `0xEA000` is
  `BOOTLOADER_REGION_START - DFU_APP_DATA_RESERVED` with
  `DFU_APP_DATA_RESERVED = 10*4096` for the nRF52840 (`Makefile`:118-120,
  `src/usb/uf2/uf2cfg.h`:21 of the 0.4.3 source RAK publishes beside the binary)
  — which is why it is quoted out of the binary and not out of the source: the
  binary carries both constants itself.

Everything above came out of the firmware that will run and the firmware that
will write it: the Walter's `components/core/flasher/` at its tag `v0.1.0` (read
with `git -C ../twinoak.meshcore.walter-firmware show v0.1.0:<path>` — nothing
checked out there and nothing written), RAK's published 0.4.3 bootloader source
at the commit `boards/base/rak4630/README.md` pins
(`d3da249c0e4df56fb972021cf6e99239f27857bf`, read with `gh api`), and the
vendored 0.4.4 binary on this disk.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The third sentence of deliverable 2 is wrong as the task sketches it, and
  §3a says the true thing.** The Walter's flasher *does* write outside the
  application region — one page, the bootloader's settings page at `0xFF000` —
  and that write is precisely what leaves a half-written radio sitting in its
  bootloader. The task's conclusion (*the radio boots this release with its key
  and settings*) holds, for a better reason than the sketch gives: what neither
  path may touch is the file system from `0xED000` up. The section above is the
  reading; §3a carries it.
- **§3a, not a renumbering.** The new section goes in as **3a** —
  `spec/FIRMWARE-MANIFEST.md` §4a and `docs/bench.md` §0a/§0b are the house form
  — because `docs/bench.md`, `docs/release.md` and three reports cite
  `docs/flashing.md` §2 and §5 by number, and renumbering a page other pages
  point into would break them for a section that belongs directly after
  *Through the Walter* anyway.
- **One stale string outside this task's deliverables was fixed.**
  `tools/linkcli.py --help` said *Speak the TwinOak box link
  (spec/LINK-PROTOCOL.md 1.0)* while the tool asks at `ref.PROTO_MINOR`, which 01
  moved to 1. It now says 1.1. One line — but it is the tool that §3a and the
  release notes send a person to, and a release that tells them 1.0 would be the

- **`docs/bench.md`'s p3.7-01 lines were left exactly as they are, unticked.**

  pretend to be a walk. That line's sentence *Its* Through the Walter *half (§3)
  cannot be walked until the Walter has a release of its own* is now out of date

  p3.7-01's line and the reviewer's to change.
- **`tools/verify.ps1` counts 394 native test cases on this tree; p3.8-01's

## Decisions taken without asking

1. **The notes say what this release is *for*, not what changed in the code.**
   `release-notes/v0.1.1.md` leads with the band and the one sentence of why —
   MeshMaster picks by role, board and band, so a radio with no golden image on
   its Walter could never be offered its first image over LTE — and then *A 0.1.0
   Walter still speaks to it*, with the mechanism a person can check:
   `requires.link_major: 1` is the only version a Walter compares, and `role`,
   `board` and `fw_version` are where they always were. A reader of a release
   body is deciding whether to flash it; that is the question answered first.
2. **The notes are honest about the board, in the same words README uses.** *Not
   this image* — but the one thing a board did show is named: `v0.1.0`'s own
   `.uf2` brought the first unit's radio back with its key, which is the event
   §3a exists for.
3. **§3a's recovery is four steps, not three.** *Reset it once* was added
   because the flasher halts the core and only its own `finish()` resets it: a
   Walter that was itself reset mid-flash never gets there, and a halted core
   runs no bootloader. A page that said *double-tap* to somebody whose board does
   nothing at all would be a page that fails on the one day it is needed.
4. **The scanner gets one sentence in the notes.** Its descriptor says
   `link 1.1` and it speaks no link, which reads as a contradiction in a release
   body; the notes say why in one sentence (a release's link version is one field
   for the whole release), as 01's report decided it.
5. **No addresses in §3a beyond the four that carry meaning** — `0x26000`,
   `0xED000`, `0xEA000`, `0xFF000`. The page is for a person with a unit on a
   desk; the derivations are in this report.
6. **The two bench lines are §0's**, beside p3.7-01's, because they are about a
   published release and its files rather than about a rig that is up — and the
   first of them says that walking it ticks p3.8-01's §2 line too, since it is
   the same reading taken off a release's file instead of a build's.
