**For the reviewer, first:** the hardware repository still holds the old copy at
`software/firmware/noisescope-rak4630/`. Removing it, and leaving a pointer to
`docs/noisescope.md` of this repository in its `PROJECT-KNOWLEDGE.md` §8, is

NoiseScope is the second role of this family. It builds here as
`scanner_rak4630_single`, from the same board, the same band and the same
Arduino pin map as the repeater; it reads the INA3221 through the repeater's own
code; it carries a descriptor whose role byte is **3**; and a release dry run

its radio bring-up — the SX1262's power-enable pin — which is under
*Deviations* and has a bench line. Done in one session.

### What the two roles share, and what they do not

| | |
|---|---|
| the image descriptor | `src/common/fwdesc/`. The `role`, `board` and `ver` of the `boot` and `info` lines are read **out of the 96 bytes**, through a new `fwdesc::roleName()` that is `roleCode()`'s inverse over the same list. That is also what keeps the linker from discarding the object — the same mechanism p3-01 measured |
| the INA3221 | `src/common/power/Ina3221.{h,cpp}`, `PowerReader.{h,cpp}` and the new `WireBus.{h,cpp}`. NoiseScope's `telemetry.cpp` had its own copy of the shift-by-three, the 40 µV and 8 mV LSBs and the three shunt values; it is gone |
| the band | `src/common/bands/`. The home channel is `LORA_FREQ/BW/SF/CR` from `[band_868]`, as the repeater's radio is, and the default sweep is the band's own range through two new `constexpr` accessors — so NoiseScope's restated `869.618/62.5/8/8` and `863.0–870.0` are read and not written |
| the version | `src/common/version/`, for `TWINOAK_FW_VERSION` and its `0.0.0-dev` default |
| **not** the mesh | the scanner compiles **not one line of MeshCore** (`lib_ignore = MeshCore`): no identity, no file system, no sensor manager, no `ADMIN_PASSWORD`, no `ENABLE_ADVERT_ON_BOOT`. The dependency graph of its build is `SPI`, `Wire`, `RadioLib` and nothing else (`out/verify/build-scanner_rak4630_single.log`) |
| **not** the radio driver | the repeater's radio is upstream's `CustomSX1262Wrapper`; the scanner's is RadioLib directly, because it retunes between LoRa and FSK, uploads a RAM patch and reads instantaneous RSSI at 1 kHz — none of which the wrapper exposes. What it keeps is the *path*: the DIO2 RF switch, the DIO3 TCXO at 1.8 V, the 140 mA limit and boosted gain, each from the same place the repeater's environment takes it from |

**The two RadioLibs are one RadioLib.** `[libs].radiolib` is now a line of its
own — `jgromes/RadioLib @ 7.8.0` — read by `${libs.core}` (the repeater) and by
`${libs.scanner}`, and the `RADIOLIB_EXCLUDE_*` list is `[radiolib]`, read by
both environments. PlatformIO installs `lib_deps` per environment
(`.pio/libdeps/<env>/`), so neither list can reach the other's build; what the
shared sections buy is that neither can *drift* from the other either. This is
the one place where the task's words and the firmware disagree, and it is under
*Deviations*.

### The task's own test list

- [x] **the command parser** — `test_scanner`'s `Commands.*` (15 tests): the command word is case-insensitive and an argument is not; repeated separators are one and a ninth word is dropped; every word of the page is a verb and `transmit`, `send` and `tx` are not; each default is the page's; both ends of a range are read together, so `sweep 865` is the default sweep; `home 868.4` retunes and leaves the modem settings alone; `boost` is on for `on` and `1` and off for anything else, including `ON`, which is NoiseScope's own reading of it; an epoch before November 2023 is refused and nothing is set; every command that can be refused has a usage line that names itself.
- [x] **the NDJSON writer (a line is valid JSON, numbers are finite)** — `test_scanner`'s `Lines.*` (10 tests). Every message is written through a string sink, split into lines, and **parsed with ArduinoJson** — the native environment's fixture reader already, and a build that rejects `nan` and `infinity`, which is exactly the refusal being tested. Then each field is held against `docs/noisescope.md`: `boot` and `info` carry `role`, `board` and `ver`; `stat` carries `rssi` only in monitor mode and `pwr` only when all three channels answered, and carries no `seq`; a `sweep` line has one value per point; `hist` has the engine's 33 bins; an empty `msg` is no field at all. And the two that are the point: a `NaN` or an infinity anywhere — including inside a `sweep` array, and including a null array pointer — comes out as `null`, the line is still JSON, and no line carries the words `nan` or `inf` where a value begins.
- [x] **the sweep's point arithmetic** — `test_scanner`'s `Sweep.*` and `Window.*` (7 and 4 tests): 863–870 MHz in 25 kHz steps is 281 points with both ends included, point 0 at `f0` and point 280 at exactly 870.0; a range that is not a whole number of steps stops at the last point inside it; the receiver bandwidth is the smallest filter at least 1.1 × the step (25 kHz → 29.3 kHz) and the widest when none is; crossed ends, a step below 1 kHz or above 1 MHz, fewer than two points, more than 512 and either end outside the radio's 150–960 MHz are each refused, and 921–925 MHz is *accepted*, because pointing the instrument at a GSM900 downlink through the cavity filter is what it is for; the four clamps; and the RSSI window — an empty one is not an average of zero, a full one counts what was above the threshold, and the next window's threshold is this one's minimum plus 10 dB and is carried over only when there was a sample.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

**The scanner's RadioLib is the registry release, not the commit MeshCore
pins.** The task says "NoiseScope pinned the commit MeshCore pins; the repeater
environment may use a registry release. Two environments may differ here — say
what each uses and why". They do not differ, and that is the decision: both are
`jgromes/RadioLib @ 7.8.0`. NoiseScope's reason for pinning `6d89348`
("7.7.1+sha") was the sentence in its own README — *the same pinned RadioLib
commit as MeshCore, so RSSI readings are comparable with what the repeater
experiences in service*. The repeater **in this repository** does not build
against that commit and cannot: MeshCore's `library.json` asks for `^7.6.0`, and
installing the commit beside that range gives two libraries for one `RadioLib.h`
(phase 0's task 01, and the `[libs]` comment). So pinning the commit in the
scanner would have made the scanner the odd one out and broken the very promise
it was meant to keep. 7.8.0 has every call this role makes, all of them public
API at that version — `uploadPatch`, `spectralScanStart/GetStatus/GetResult/
Abort`, `setRxBoostedGainMode`, `scanChannel`, `getRSSI(bool)` and the patch
header `modules/SX126x/patches/SX126x_patch_scan.h`
(`.pio/libdeps/<env>/RadioLib/src/modules/SX126x/SX126x.h:489, 768, 777, 782,
788, 795`) — and `RADIOLIB_SX126X_SPECTRAL_SCAN_RES_SIZE` is still 33, which a
`static_assert` in `Radio.cpp` now holds against our own `kHistBins`.

**One cannot leak into the other** by construction and is held so on purpose:
PlatformIO installs `lib_deps` per environment, the two lists are
`${libs.core}`-plus-MeshCore and `${libs.scanner}`, and the one line each reads
the version from is `[libs].radiolib`. The `RADIOLIB_EXCLUDE_*` list moved out of
our copy of upstream's `[nrf52_base]` into `[radiolib]` for the same reason;
`docs/upstream.md`'s first row names it, so a bump re-reads it there.

**The scanner's `build_src_filter` is a list and not `+<common/>`.** The task,
and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §8 as p3-01 wrote it, say a role's environment
compiles `+<common/> +<roles/<role>/>`. Three files of `common/` are MeshCore's
side of the build and cannot be in an image that has no MeshCore:
`board/target.cpp` (which exists in order to *be* upstream's `<target.h>`),
`link/SerialTransport.cpp` (the box link's frames, which this role does not
speak) and `power/BoardPower.cpp` (which keeps a preferences file in the internal
flash — the very thing rule 11 forbids). So the scanner names the files of
`common/` it uses, the way `[env:native]` does, and a file it needs and did not
name does not link. `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §8 has a new subsection, *What a role
compiles of `common/`*, and the *Adding a role* list now says it.

**The scanner defines none of the six `RoleHooks.h` functions.** p3-01's rule is
"a role defines all six and there is no weak default, so a role that forgets one
does not link". That rule is intact and it is why the scanner needs none: the
only two files that *call* a hook are `board/target.cpp` and
`power/BoardPower.cpp`, and neither is in this build. Writing six stubs would
have been six functions nothing can reach. The *Adding a role* list now says
"every function of `RoleHooks.h` that the files of `common/` the environment
compiles reach" instead of "all six".

**`docs/bench.md`'s new section is §9 and not §8.** The task says *Add to
`docs/bench.md`, "8. NoiseScope"*; §8 is p3-01's *What an image says it is*.

**`stat` carries no `seq`.** `PROTOCOL.md` said "Every data line carries `seq`",
and NoiseScope's own `emit_stat` did not — its `stat` example has no `seq`
either. The firmware's behaviour is unchanged and `docs/noisescope.md` now says
what it actually is: every line a scan produces carries `seq`, `boot` carries
`ts`, and `stat` carries `ts` and `ms`, because a heartbeat is not a
measurement. The `info` line gained no `mode` field for the same reason — the
page did not promise one.

**`src/common/power/WireBus.{h,cpp}` is new, and `BoardPower.cpp` is 50 lines
shorter.** The fourth criterion above is the whole accounting of what that did
to the repeater's image.

## Decisions taken without asking

1. **The SX1262's power-enable pin is driven high.** This is the one change to
   NoiseScope's behaviour, and it is deliberate. `SX126X_POWER_EN` is pin 37
   (P1.05) in MeshCore's `variants/rak4631/variant.h:155`, and
   `RAK4631Board.cpp:58-66` at the pin is `pinMode(..., OUTPUT)`, the boot-voltage
   check, `digitalWrite(..., HIGH)` and `delay(10)` with upstream's own comment
   *give sx1262 some time to power up*; `initiateShutdown` drives it low again
   before the unit sleeps. After a reset that GPIO is an input, so the radio of
   this stamp is unpowered until something drives the pin — and **NoiseScope
   never named it**. Its README promises the repeater's RX path, and a radio with
   no 3V3 is not that path; `PROJECT-KNOWLEDGE.md` of the hardware repository
   lists "flash NoiseScope, `info` shows RTC=1" as a bench step still to be
   walked, so no board has ever contradicted the reading. The smallest decision
   consistent with the spec was to make the bring-up the repeater's, cite the
   three lines it came from, and give the bench a line that says what to look at
   if `radio_ok` is false anyway.
2. **The MCU's DC/DC converter is left alone.** The repeater's board calls
   MeshCore's `NRF52BoardDCDC::begin()`; that helper is MeshCore and this image
   has none of it, and writing our own would be a change to the supply the radio
   sits on with nothing to measure it against. `docs/noisescope.md` §6 records it
   as a known difference and `docs/bench.md` §9 says where it would show.
3. **MeshCore is in the scanner's `lib_deps` and ignored.** The image compiles
   not one line of it, but the pinned checkout has to be on disk: `variant.h` and
   `variant.cpp` are upstream's for this board (above), and the UF2 converter the
   release needs is read out of `bin/uf2conv/uf2conv.py` by
   `tools/pio/meshcore_uf2.py`. `lib_ignore = MeshCore` keeps the library out of
   the build entirely — `build_as_lib.py` never runs, and the build's dependency
   graph is three libraries — while `pio pkg install` still clones it. The
   alternative was a second UF2 converter of our own, which would have meant the
   two images' `.uf2` files were made two different ways.
4. **Three portable files, not one.** NoiseScope had the command parser, the
   output and the arithmetic inside `main.cpp` and `scanner.cpp`, interleaved
   with radio calls. The split is `Commands`, `Lines` and `Sweep` — no Arduino,
   no RadioLib, compiled by `[env:native]` — with `Radio.cpp` left as the part
   that touches the part. That is this repository's own rule
   (`tasks/README.md`: logic is portable, the board-facing file is thin) and it
   is what made the task's test list possible at all.
5. **A number that is not a number is `null`.** The task asks the NDJSON test to
   show that "numbers are finite". A `%.1f` of a NaN prints `nan`, which is not
   JSON, so the *line* would be dropped by a reader and everything else on it
   lost. Every number now goes through one function that writes `null` instead.
   NoiseScope could emit `nan` — a window with no sample was guarded, a radio
   that answered nothing was not.
6. **The monitor's and the dwell's accumulators are one class.** NoiseScope kept
   two copies of the same arithmetic with the same threshold carry-over.
   `RssiWindow` is the one of them, and the carry-over rule — including "a window
   with no sample carries nothing over" — has a test.
7. **`double` where NoiseScope had `float`, and no deci-dBm trick.** The sweep's
   accumulators are `double avg[512]` and `double max[512]` where NoiseScope had
   `float acc[512]` and `int16_t mx10[512]` — 8 KB of RAM instead of 3 KB, on a
   part with 230 KB and an image using 20 KB of it. The int16 max-hold lost a
   digit it then printed; the portable arithmetic is written in `double` because
   that is what a host test compares exactly. Nothing visible changes: every
   number is printed with the same precision the page documents.
8. **`stat`'s power numbers come from the reader's cache.** NoiseScope read six
   INA3221 registers inside `emit_stat`. `src/common/power/PowerReader` samples
   one channel per `loop()` on a one-second schedule with a back-off, so a `stat`
   never waits on I2C — the repeater's rule, and the reason the same code could
   be shared at all. During a long sweep the values are the last ones taken;
   `docs/noisescope.md` says so.
9. **Two header wrappers.** `src/common/board/CoreHeaders.h` is the Arduino
   core's `Arduino.h` and `Wire.h` as system headers, and `UpstreamHeaders.h`
   includes it and adds everything above the core. Without the split,
   `WireBus.cpp` — a file of `common/` that needs only `Wire` — would have pulled
   MeshCore, RadioLib and upstream's board class into a build that has none of
   them; with it, the invariant that no file of ours includes an upstream header
   outside a system-header wrapper still holds, and `ScannerHeaders.h` is the
   role's own third one.
10. **The role's own name comes from the descriptor, through a new
    `fwdesc::roleName()`.** `imageRole()` is a byte and the NDJSON lines need the
    word. The inverse of `roleCode()` over the same list is six lines, keeps the
    "readers read the object" discipline p3-01 set — which is also what keeps the
    descriptor linked into the image — and has a round-trip test.
11. **Two `constexpr` accessors on the band table.** `bands::lowOf()` and
    `highOf()` exist so that the scanner's default sweep is the band's own range
    rather than a restated `863.0`/`870.0`. A band the table does not know has no
    range and answers 0 MHz, which is no frequency at all.
12. **Rule 3 of the rails now applies to the environments that compile
    upstream's example.** `ADMIN_PASSWORD` has to be defined where upstream's
    `MyMesh.cpp` would otherwise default it to the word every stock repeater
    ships with; an environment with no `BUILD_EXAMPLE` has no admin and no mesh,
    and is held to the other side of the same rule instead — it must not define
    the password at all, so one cannot arrive in an image with nowhere to put it.
    The test also fails if no board environment compiles upstream's repeater any
    more, so the rail cannot quietly stop checking anything.
13. **The scanner has no `433` and no second board**, for the same reason the
    repeater has none: a band or a board nobody has measured a radio against is
    not shipped by being named.

## New libraries (with the one-line justification)

None. `jgromes/RadioLib @ 7.8.0` is already a dependency at that exact version;
the scanner's list is that line plus `SPI` and `Wire`, and
`tools/noisescope/requirements.txt` (pyserial, matplotlib, numpy) is a

## Not verified here (and why)

The same six lines are now `docs/bench.md` §9, in the order the rig is brought
up. No session can flash a board or hear a radio (RUNBOOK §2); everything below
needs one, and the fifth is the one the whole task rests on.

- **Flash the scanner over USB and read its `boot` line.** `fw` is
  `noisescope`, `role` is `scanner`, `board` is `rak4630-single`, and
  **`radio_ok`** is the number to write down. If it is false, `SX126X_POWER_EN`
  is the first thing to look at: this port drives it high where NoiseScope did
  not, from a reading of `RAK4631Board.cpp` and not from a measurement
  (*Decisions* 1).
- **`info`, `stat` and `sweep` behave as `docs/noisescope.md` says, on the USB
  console and on `Serial1`.** A `sweep` of 281 points in about 7 s, a `hist`
  that uploads Semtech's RAM patch and comes back with 33 bins, a `dwell`, a
  `cad`, and a `stop` in the middle of a pass — which must be answered between
  steps and not after it. The commands, the lines and the arithmetic are tested
  natively; what no session can show is the radio answering at all.
- **`pwr` matches the repeater's `power` numbers for the same board**
  (`[boards]`). The two now read the INA3221 through the same code, so a
  disagreement here would be between two runs and not between two firmwares —
  which is exactly why it is worth walking once.
- **Every line of a minute's real output parses, and none carries `nan`.**
  `tools/noisescope/waterfall_plot.py` drops anything not starting with `{`, so
  it is also the check. The writer is held to this natively; a real radio's
  readings going through it — including a window with no sample — are not.
- **Flash the repeater back and find nothing of the node gone.** The same
