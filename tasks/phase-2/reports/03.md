# p2-03 — observations

Every advert this repeater hears — after its signature verified — is now kept for the Walter: a
ring of 64 records of `LINK-PROTOCOL.md` §7, with the path the advert arrived by, its SNR, its
RSSI and the application data **copied whole and never parsed**. `OBS_PULL` hands them over oldest
first, as many as fit one payload, and the records it hands out leave the ring; a link-level retry
gets the same ones back. `STATUS`'s `obs_pending`/`obs_dropped` and `MESH_COUNTERS`' `adverts`,
`adverts_dropped` and `obs_dropped` are filled from it, and the note `logRx` takes now also fills
the `rssi` of task 02's reply record. `HELLO`'s `caps` is **`0x7f`** — every bit protocol 1.0
defines, which is every capability this repeater role has. Not one file of upstream's is touched.
Done in one session.

## The ring's RAM, measured

From the linker map (`.pio/build/repeater_rak4630_single/firmware.map`) and from
`arm-none-eabi-g++ -mcpu=cortex-m4` over the headers:

| | bytes | |
|---|---:|---|
| `HeardAdvert` | **144** | 32 key + 4 `adv_ts` + 4 `heard_ms` + 64 path + 32 app_data + 2 rssi + 1 snr + 1 `path_len` + 1 `app_len` = 141, and 3 of alignment at the end |
| `ObservationRing` (`g_ring` in `.bss`) | **9236** | 64 × 144 = 9216, plus the two ring indices and the four counters |
| `LinkObservations` (`g_observations`) | **192** | the five `link::Observation` views one answer can hold (5 × 36 = 180), the ring reference, `obs.wake` and the armed flag |
| `RssiNotes`, inside the `TwinOakMesh` | **196** | 32 packet pointers and 32 `int16_t`, one per entry of this build's packet pool |
| | **9624** | and the measured 9632 is that plus the alignment of three objects in `.bss` |

That is **3.9 %** of the 235 520 bytes this board has, and the node sits at 19.2 % with it. The
flash is the ring, the pull's fitter, the `OBS_PULL` handler, `logRx`'s note and the record built
in `onAdvertRecv`, plus `encodeObsPullResponse` and `decodeObsPull` linked at last (`--gc-sections`
had dropped both).

Sixty-four records is what the task asks for and what the agent firmware's ring holds; §7 asks for
at least 32. At 64 the ring is three quarters full after 48 adverts, which on a busy Danish window
is well under an hour — so the wake threshold matters, and it is off by default (below).

## What is where

- **`src/meshclient/ObservationRing.{h,cpp}`** (portable, in the native filter): the record of §7
  and the ring. `push` counts every advert that verified, refuses a record §7 cannot carry
  (counting it as an advert dropped), and on a full ring overwrites the **oldest** and counts the
  loss twice — since boot (`STATUS`, `MESH_COUNTERS`) and since the last pull (§7's `dropped`),
  both saturating. `at(0)` is the oldest; `consume(n)` is what a pull costs it. `ageSeconds()` is
  §7's `age_s`, measured on the millisecond clock so that a `TIME_SET` cannot move it.
- **`src/meshclient/RssiNotes.h`** (portable, header-only): the RSSI of a packet, noted in `logRx`
  and taken back once. It exists because a packet's **SNR** is the packet's own
  (`Dispatcher::checkRecv`, `pkt->_snr = _radio->getLastSNR() * 4.0f`, `src/Dispatcher.cpp:206`)
  but its RSSI is not — the radio keeps one "last RSSI" and a flood packet is deliberately held
  back for up to `MAX_RX_DELAY_MILLIS`, 32 seconds (`:243-253`, `queueInbound(pkt,
  futureMillis(_delay))`), by which time several other frames have arrived. The table is 32
  entries, this build's packet pool.
- **`src/meshclient/LinkObservations.{h,cpp}`** (portable): the ring as the link's
  `ObservationAccess`. Three things are decided here — how many records fit one payload, what a
  pull costs the ring, and when a filling ring may ask for a sleeping Walter.
- **`src/app/TwinOakMesh.{h,cpp}`**: `logRx` calls the base and then notes the radio's last RSSI
  against the packet; `onAdvertRecv` **calls the base first** and then builds the record and hands
  it to the ring. The three peer overrides pass `rssiOf(packet)` where task 02 passed
  `kReplyRssiUnknown`.
- **`src/link/session/`**: `ObservationAccess` grew the pull and the three counts; the session has
  the `OBS_PULL` handler and fills `MESH_COUNTERS`' three advert fields.
- **`src/link/BoardLink.{h,cpp}`**: the one `ObservationRing` and its `LinkObservations`, wired to
  the session (=> `kCapObservations`), and `noteAdvert()` — the one line that joins the ring's
  threshold to the attention machine's hourly limit.
- **`src/app/Settings.{h,cpp}`** and **`src/board/target.cpp`**: an eighth key, `obs.wake`,
  appended so that nothing already documented moved. `sensor list 5` now prints three.
- **`tools/linkcli.py`**: `obs [max]` and `--names`.
- **`tools/linkloop/`**: the real ring behind the pipe, with `--adverts <n>` and `--obs-wake`;
  `check.sh` drives `obs`, a full ring and a pull of nothing.
- **Docs**: `docs/link.md` §2g (the whole of observations) with `caps` and the message list at
  `0x7f`; `docs/bench.md` §7; `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §4's settings table, §7 and §10;
  `docs/upstream.md` place 5 (the new citations).

## Where the pin was read, and what it says

Read from the pinned checkout (`d9296435`, `repeater-v1.17.1`); the agent firmware's lines are at
`51faafb4`.

- **`onAdvertRecv` is only ever called for an advert that verified, and never for our own.**
  `Mesh::onRecvPacket`'s advert branch drops a self advert at `src/Mesh.cpp:263`
  (`self_id.matches(id.pub_key)`), de-duplicates against `_tables->wasSeen`, verifies the Ed25519
  signature, and only then calls `onAdvertRecv` (`:284`). So "an advert from this node's own key is
  not recorded" is already upstream's behaviour; ours says it again in one line rather than resting
  on it.
- **`app_data` is already held to 32 bytes.** `Mesh.cpp:269` clamps `app_data_len` to
  `MAX_ADVERT_DATA_SIZE`, which is **32** (`src/MeshCore.h:12`) — the same number §7 caps `app_len`
  at. A `static_assert` in `TwinOakMesh.cpp` holds the two together, so nothing is ever truncated
  on this pin and a bump that raises upstream's is a compile error here.
- **A path always fits 64 bytes.** `Dispatcher::tryParsePacket` refuses path mode 3 outright
  (`src/Dispatcher.cpp:166-170`) and refuses a packet whose `(path_len & 63) * hash_size` exceeds
  `MAX_PATH_SIZE` (`:172-176`). So a record's path is at most 64 bytes, which is what the record
  holds.
- **The RSSI really does have to be noted in `logRx`.** `checkRecv` stores the SNR in the packet
  (`:206`) but nothing stores the RSSI; it calls `logRx` (`:238`) and then, for a flood packet whose
  score delay is 50 ms or more, queues it for later (`:252`) with the delay capped at
  `MAX_RX_DELAY_MILLIS` = 32 000 (`:11`). The agent firmware's `AgentMesh::logRx` comment says "a
  flood frame can wait in the inbound queue for up to a second"; at this pin the cap is 32 seconds,
  which makes the case stronger, not weaker. That is the only place the two differ.
- **The packet pool is 32, and it is not a macro.** `MyMesh`'s constructor passes
  `*new StaticPoolPacketManager(32)` (`MyMesh.cpp:863`); the agent firmware's table is 16 because
  its own `main.cpp` builds a pool of 16. There is no name to assert against, so
  `TwinOakMesh.cpp` asserts `kRssiNotes == 32` against a literal and cites the line — a bump that
  resizes the pool fails there rather than silently shortening the table.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The fixture's `OBS_PULL` response is a *shape*, not the answer to the request case beside
  it.** `spec/fixtures/link/link-v1.json`'s "observations pull" (seq 35) asks `max` 4; the response
  with the same seq carries `count` 2 with `remaining` 5. No ring can produce that from that
  request *and* hand out as many as it has: two records of 64 and 56 bytes leave 115 bytes of the
  235 a payload has for records, so a third and a fourth would have fitted. §7's wording is an
  upper bound — "as many as fit in one payload and no more than `max`" — so the fixture is legal and
  our firmware, which returns as many as it can, is legal too; they simply are not the same answer.
  So the case is tested as the shape it is: byte for byte through the real `Session` with the
  fixture's own records scripted into the ring interface
  (`TheFixtures.EveryRequestThisTaskAnswersGivesTheFixturesResponseFrame`, 13 request types now),
  and byte for byte through the encoder in `test_link_codec` as before. What the **real** ring hands
  out, in what order and how many, is five further tests through a real session
  (`Observations.*`) and eight against the ring itself (`TheRing.*`, `ThePull.*`). Worth a line in
  the specification if anybody ever reads the fixture as a worked example; nothing in the firmware
  changes either way.
- **`MESH_COUNTERS`' `obs_dropped` is read from `observationsDropped()` and not from the
  `droppedSinceBoot()` `STATUS` uses.** They are the same count; §5.3's field is a saturating `u16`
  and §6.4's is a `u32`, so the interface answers both and the session takes the wider one for the
  wider field. A node that lost more than 65 535 observations would read 65 535 in `STATUS` and the
  true number in `MESH_COUNTERS`, which is what the two field widths mean.
- **`adverts_dropped` cannot be anything but 0 on this pin.** §6.4 lists it beside `adverts`; the
  agent firmware counts an advert whose `app_data` did not decode, and this firmware does not decode
  `app_data` at all (that is the point — §7 and S-12). What is left to refuse is a record §7 cannot
  carry, and the three bounds that would cause one are all held by MeshCore's own parser and by the
  `static_assert`s above. So the counter is a **backstop against a pin bump** and reads 0 on a
  healthy node of this build. It is counted and tested all the same
  (`TheRing.ARecordItCannotCarryIsCountedAndNotKept`), because the day it is not 0 is the day
  somebody needs to know.
- **`src/link/session/Interfaces.h`'s `ObservationAccess` grew four methods** — `pull` and the
  three counts of §6.4. A null `observations` pointer is still `kCapObservations` absent,
  `kErrUnsupported` for `OBS_PULL`, and zero in both `STATUS` fields and all three counters
  (`Observations.WithNoRingObsPullIsUnsupportedAndTheCountsReadZero`).
- **No other `spec/` disagreement was found.**

## Decisions taken without asking

- **`obs.wake` is per-boot RAM, not a saved preference.** The task asks for a setting with a
  default of off and says nothing about persistence. Saving it would mean a field in
  `src/power/Prefs.h`'s record, whose own comment reserves its flags byte "behind a new magic" — and
  a new magic makes the v1 record unreadable, so every unit would lose the battery thresholds
  somebody had set. Per-boot costs the Walter one request after each `EV_BOOT`, which it already
  owes for the clock (§5.4, "before anything else it asks the RAK to do"), and a reset falls back to
  *off*, which is the safe direction. `src/app/Settings.h` says so where the key is defined.
- **Reason 5 is asked for once per filling of the ring, and re-armed by a pull that takes it back
  below the threshold.** §8 says "the observation ring is nearly full"; the task says "when the ring
  passes three quarters full". Asking on every advert above 48 and letting the hourly limit do the
  work would wake a Walter every hour for as long as nobody collected; asking once per crossing
  wakes it once and leaves the hourly limit as the second backstop. One subtlety is deliberate: a
  ring that crosses the threshold while the Walter is believed **awake** does not spend the ask — it
  stays owed, so the first advert after a `SLEEP_NOTICE` raises it
  (`TheWakeThreshold.ItIsTheCrossingAndOnlyWhileTheWalterIsBelievedAsleep`).
- **The threshold is three quarters of the ring's own capacity**, `kObsCapacity - kObsCapacity / 4`
  = 48 of 64, computed and not written out, so a ring of another size moves it.
- **A pull with `max` 0 still reports `dropped` and resets it.** §7 is silent; it is an `OBS_PULL`,
  and `dropped` is "lost since the last `OBS_PULL`". It takes no records and drains nothing
  (`ThePull.FewerThanThereAreMoreThanThereAreAndZero`).
- **The ring and its link face are globals in `src/link/BoardLink.cpp`, not members of
  `TwinOakMesh`.** 9.2 KB belongs in `.bss` beside the session that empties it rather than inside
  the mesh object, and `noteAdvert()` needs the attention machine, which lives in that same file.
  `TwinOakMesh` keeps only the 196-byte note table, which is the one piece that is about packets.
- **The pull's records are views into the ring's own storage, not a copy.** `consume()` only moves
  the ring's indices, so the bytes the five staged `link::Observation`s point at are still there
  when the session encodes them — which it does inside the same handler call, and the mesh's loop
  and the link's are the same loop, so nothing can push a new advert in between. Copying them
  instead would have cost another 720 bytes of RAM for no property gained. Both
  `LinkObservations.h` and `Interfaces.h` say how long the views live.
- **The fitter uses the codec's own `obsPathBytes()`**, which is the same function
  `encodeObsPullResponse` checks each record's path against, so a record the fitter admits is one
  the encoder will take; and the header it reserves (5 bytes) is held against the encoder by a test
  (`ThePull.TheHeaderIsTheFiveBytesTheFitterAssumes`) rather than by arithmetic repeated in two
  places. The ring's own bound uses the job's `pathHashSize`/`pathHopCount`, and a test holds the
  two formulas together for all 256 values of `path_len`.
- **`onTraceRecv` takes the note for any trace that ends here, not only an awaited one.** The note
  is consumed whichever way the client judges the trace, because a TRACE is never an advert and
  nothing else wants it. Simpler than threading the decision back out of the client, and it cannot
  cost an observation its RSSI.
- **`HeardAdvert`'s fields are in descending alignment** so that the only padding in the record is
  the three bytes its own alignment adds at the end. At 64 records that choice is worth 256 bytes.
- **`obs [max]` in `linkcli.py` defaults to 255** — "as many as you can" — and `--names` is
  opt-in, because the name is the one field a person has to ask for by name.
- **The loopback's adverts are synthetic and built by `linkloop.cpp`**, half of them direct
  neighbours with an RSSI and half two hops away without one, because nothing on a host can hear a
  radio. `--adverts 70` is how a full ring, a loss count and §7's `dropped` are watched from a
  laptop.

## Not verified here (and why)

Added to `docs/bench.md` as section **7. Observations**, tagged **p2-03**; `docs/link.md` §2g is
the page that walks it. It needs **ten minutes on the roof antenna** and nothing else — no second
radio and no companion, because the mesh advertises by itself:

- `obs` lists the repeaters in range with plausible hops, SNR and RSSI, and a direct neighbour has
  hops 0 and an RSSI; `obs --names` shows the names, decoded on the laptop.
- `neighbors` on the unit's own console still lists its neighbours as before — the one thing that
  would go wrong if `onAdvertRecv` did not call its base first.
- A pull empties what it returns and a retry does not.
- With the laptop away for a day, `status` shows the ring full with a loss count and the repeater is
  none the worse.
- A reply's `rssi` is a number now and not *unknown* (the line task 02 left owed).
- `obs.wake` on: reason 5 within one advert of crossing 48 while the Walter is believed asleep, at
  most once an hour; off — the default — raises nothing however full the ring gets.

And, not on the bench list because no rig is involved:

- **`logRx` and `onAdvertRecv` never ran against MeshCore on a host.** They need the real
  `mesh::Mesh` and a real `Packet`, which a native build does not have. What is tested natively is
  every piece they are made of — the ring, the pull's fitting, the age arithmetic, the note table
  (a pooled packet reused, a note taken back once, a note pushed out of a full table) — and the
  `OBS_PULL`, `STATUS` and `MESH_COUNTERS` paths through a real `Session` over the real ring. The
  two overrides themselves are checked by review against the pinned source, as tasks 01 and 02's
  were.
- **`tools/linkloop/check.sh` on a machine with its own `g++`** ran in the container here, as in
  every task since phase 1; the host path of that script has still never run.
