# p2-04 — the rails

A mistake on the Walter can no longer turn this repeater into a nuisance on the mesh: the RAK
enforces a **ceiling of 6 client sends in any 60 seconds, at least 2 seconds apart**, refuses what
is over it with `ERR_BUSY` before anything is built, and counts the refusals in
`sensor get client.refused`. Every rule the project states now has a test that fails with a
sentence naming the rule — **nine rules, eleven tests** in `test/test_rails`, each of them broken
on purpose once in this session with the sentence quoted below. The two questions the task asks
about upstream's transmit path are answered out of the pinned source and written into
`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §7: **the duty cycle covers our packets too, and a client send cannot delay
a forward by more than its own airtime** — so nothing of our own is queued and nothing is proposed.
`docs/bench.md` is one ordered walk of 58 lines with a legend, a `[boards]` mark and blanks for the
numbers; `docs/link.md` has a request-to-capability table and a section on the two ceilings;
`README.md` says what the firmware is today and names the test behind each rail. Not one file of
upstream's or of `spec/` is touched. Done in one session.

## The two questions about upstream, and the lines they were read from

Read from the pinned checkout (`d9296435`, `repeater-v1.17.1`). Both answers are in
`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §7 as well, which is where a reader will look for them.

**1. Does upstream's duty-cycle accounting cover a locally originated packet?** Yes, and there is
nothing of ours beside it.

- `Dispatcher::sendPacket` (`src/Dispatcher.cpp:371-378`) is the only way into the outbound queue,
  and both `Mesh::sendDirect`/`sendFlood`/`sendZeroHop` (our sends) and
  `Dispatcher::processRecvPacket`'s `ACTION_RETRANSMIT*` (every forward, `:261-273`) go through it.
- `Dispatcher::checkSend` (`:275-354`) is the only way out of it. Before it takes anything it calls
  `updateTxBudget()` and **returns without transmitting** while `tx_budget_ms` is below
  `est_airtime/MIN_TX_BUDGET_AIRTIME_DIV` — half the estimated airtime of a `MAX_TRANS_UNIT`
  packet (`:279-286`).
- When a send completes, `Dispatcher::loop` subtracts its **measured** airtime from the budget
  (`:88-98`, `long t = getMillis() - outbound_start; … tx_budget_ms -= t`) and pushes `next_tx_time`
  out if that leaves less than `MIN_TX_BUDGET_RESERVE_MS` = 100 ms (`:99-102`). Nothing in either
  path asks who originated the packet.
- The budget is half the window **by this repeater's own default, and it is settable**: the base's
  `Dispatcher::getAirtimeBudgetFactor()` returns 1.0 (`:34-36`), but `MyMesh` **overrides** it to
  return `_prefs.airtime_factor` (`examples/simple_repeater/MyMesh.h:134-137`), its constructor sets
  that to `1.0` (`MyMesh.cpp:890`), and the console's `set dutycycle <1..100>` writes
  `airtime_factor = 100/dc - 1` and saves it (`src/helpers/CommonCLI.cpp:450-461`; `set af <x>`
  writes it directly, and `get dutycycle` prints `100/(af+1)` as a percentage, `:809-814`). So at
  the default `duty_cycle = 1/(1+1)` and `tx_budget_ms` fills to `getDutyCycleWindowMs() * 0.5` =
  **1 800 000 ms of a 3 600 000 ms window** (`Dispatcher.cpp:25-27`, `:38-52`; the window is
  `Dispatcher.h:173`'s 3 600 000).

So the two limits are about different things, which is what the report should say: upstream's is
the only one that can hold the band's limit, because it is the only one that sees the hundreds of
packets a busy repeater forwards; ours is about the mesh's patience, and is a number a correct
Walter never reaches. For size: at 869.618 MHz / 62.5 kHz / SF8 / CR8 with the 32 preamble symbols
`RadioLibWrapper::preambleLengthForSF` sets for SF ≤ 8, one 186-byte packet is **about 1.7 s** of
airtime and a 246-byte one (a datagram sent direct along a long path) about 2.2 s, so six of them
is some 10-13 s a minute - **17 to 22 %** against the default 50 %. A real exchange is much
smaller: a login is about 0.64 s and a status request 0.7 s, so the server's own 2 a minute is
about 2 % of the hour.

**The one interaction worth knowing, since the duty cycle is settable.** A unit somebody has set
to `dutycycle 10` has a budget of 360 s an hour; six maximum-size sends a minute sustained would
be some 790 s, so a run-away Walter on such a unit could saturate upstream's budget as well as
meet our ceiling. What happens then is not a loss: `checkSend` pushes `next_tx_time` out and the
queue waits (`:279-286`), which is the same thing that happens to a busy repeater's own forwards.
The ceiling is still what stops the Walter, six a minute is still the most it can ask for, and
nothing of this changes on a unit left at the default. It is worth a line on the bench if anybody
ever sets a low duty cycle on a unit with an exit node on it.

**2. How is a locally originated packet queued against packets waiting to be forwarded, and can a
client send delay a forward by more than its own airtime?** It cannot.

- The queue is one flat table of at most `pool_size` entries, picked over on every `get()`:
  **lowest priority number first, and among equal priorities the lowest index — which is the one
  added first** (`PacketQueue::get`, `src/helpers/StaticPoolPacketManager.cpp:22-44`; `add` appends
  at `_num`, `:60-68`). Entries scheduled for the future are skipped.
- The priorities, all at this pin:

  | what | priority |
  |---|---|
  | a forward of routed (direct) traffic | **0** — `ACTION_RETRANSMIT_DELAYED(0, d)`, "Routed traffic is HIGHEST priority" (`src/Mesh.cpp:106`) |
  | **our direct sends** — a login, a request, a CLI line along a path | **0** (`Mesh::sendDirect`, `:693-715`; 1 for a PATH payload) |
  | a forward of a flood | **the hop count after this node's hash is appended**: 1 from a direct neighbour, more as it propagates (`Mesh::routeRecvPacket`, `:344-357`) |
  | **our floods** — a login or a request with no path, and every discovery | **1** (`Mesh::sendFlood`, `:637-662`; 2 for a PATH, 3 for an advert) |
  | a forward of a trace, and **our traces** | **5** (`:64` and `:698-704`) |

- So a send of ours **never jumps a forward already in the queue at the same priority** (ties go to
  whichever was added first), and it is ahead only of a flood forward that has already travelled
  two hops or more. Being ahead costs that forward exactly the airtime of our packet: one packet
  leaves, then `get()` picks again.
- Two things that could have made it worse, and do not. While a send is in flight
  `Dispatcher::loop` returns early (`:126`, "can't do any more radio activity until send is
  complete") so nothing else is processed — but that is true of every packet upstream sends, and a
  transmitting radio could not be receiving anyway. And the `MIN_TX_BUDGET_RESERVE_MS` push-out
  above is 100 ms out of 1 800 000, reachable only by a node that has transmitted for half the
  hour.
- **Nothing can starve forwarding, so no fix is proposed** and no queue of our own was written
  (the task says to stop short of one, and there is nothing to stop short of). What *would* need
  one is a client that could queue several packets at once — which the ceiling's 2-second gap also
  forbids.
- One shared resource is worth naming because it is **not** the queue: the packet **pool** is 32
  (`*new StaticPoolPacketManager(32)`, `examples/simple_repeater/MyMesh.cpp:863`) and a client send
  holds one entry from the moment it is built until the radio has sent it; a received packet that
  finds the pool empty is dropped (`Dispatcher::checkRecv`, `src/Dispatcher.cpp:201-203`). With 2
  seconds between our sends and ~1.7 s of airtime for the longest, ours holds one entry at a time
  in anything but a pathological case, and the pathological case is a radio that cannot transmit.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **6 a minute and 2 seconds apart, as build constants with their reasoning in the header.** The
  task gives both numbers; what it leaves open is *why* they are safe, and the header says it from
  two sides — three times the server's default rate of `2/min gap 20 s` (read out of
  twinoak/meshmaster-agent-firmware's `README.md` at `51faafb4`, the `server` and `jobs` console
  screens, so the figure is the server's own and not a memory of it), and 17–22 % of airtime
  against upstream's 50 % duty cycle. They are `#ifndef`-guarded so a bench can override one from
  `platformio.ini` without editing a file, and are **not** settings: a limit the Walter could raise
  would be no backstop against the Walter.
- **The window is a ring of six timestamps, not a token bucket.** "At most 6 in **any** 60
  seconds" is exactly "fewer than six of the last six sends are inside the window", which six
  32-bit words answer without arithmetic that drifts. It also means the ceiling behaves as a
  sliding window rather than a fixed minute: after a burst of six, one more send is admitted as
  each of them leaves the window, and a test walks that re-forming burst
  (`TheCeiling.TheWindowSlides`).
- **A refused send costs nothing.** It does not enter the window and does not restart the gap, so
  a Walter in a loop does not push its own next legitimate send further away — the opposite choice
  would turn a bug on the Walter into a unit that cannot be used at all. Tested with a thousand
  refusals (`TheCeiling.ARefusedSendCostsNothing`).
- **`ERR_BUSY` for the refusal, and `STATUS`'s flag bit 1 is how the Walter tells the two causes
  apart.** §6.2 has no status for "too often" and `ERR_BUSY` is its word for "not now"; bit 1 is
  set for the one cause and clear for the other, which costs the Walter nothing and needed no new
  field. `docs/link.md` §2h and `tools/linkcli.py`'s own message say so.
- **`client.refused` is read-only and saturating.** The counter is a `u32` that stops at
  `0xFFFFFFFF`; a `set` of it is refused like any read-only key, because the thing it counts is a
  build constant.
- **The rails test is one `TEST` per rule, named `Rule<N>_…`**, with the two rails of earlier tasks
  that are the same kind of thing kept beside them as `AlsoKept_…`. The task asks for "one test,
  one line per rule"; three of the nine rules need two or three greps to be honest (rule 1 is
  `platformio.ini` *and* `src/`; rule 2 is three directories; rules 3 and 8 are two halves each), so
  eleven tests carry nine numbers and the name of each says which rule it holds. The header comment
  lists all nine with **what each does and does not catch**.
- **Rule 6 is scoped to the four places the bytes actually pass through** — `src/link/codec/`,
  `src/link/session/`, `src/meshclient/` and `src/app/TwinOakMesh.{h,cpp}` — and is a ban on naming
  a file-system API at all, not a ban on writing one. That is the same shape as the I2C rail of
  phase 1's task 06: one directory may reach the thing, and the ones on the sensitive path may not
  name it. `src/app/TwinOakMesh.cpp` is on the list and passes, which is worth knowing because it
  includes upstream's world and could have reached the file system by accident.
- **Rule 8's second half carries an allowlist of the three scripts that may read the pinned
  checkout.** A per-line grep for a write cannot catch a path put in a variable on one line and
  written on the next; naming the three scripts that reach into it at all means a fourth is a
  failure until somebody has thought about it. `docs/upstream.md` already lists those three among
  the places a bump re-reads.
- **`docs/bench.md`'s legend is `[boards]` and `→ ______`.** The task asks for lines that need the
  production boards to be marked and for room for a number. The rig the rest can be walked on is
  the hardware repository's `BENCH-BREADBOARD.md` §1 — a RAK4631 Core on a RAK19001 base — so the
  legend also says the two things that differ there: the link lands on P0.15/P0.16 of the base
  rather than the adapter's J4/J5, and the current-sense breakout has one shunt value where the
  board has three. Five lines are `[boards]`: the bulkhead filters, the three current channels, the
  phone's telemetry view, the enclosure's air, and the INA3221 unplugged.
- **The deliberate breaks were applied to the working tree and reverted, not to a branch of their
  own**, because the rails read files and need no build: that makes each break a two-second run
  instead of a two-minute one, and the script keeps the original bytes in `out/tmp/backup/` while
  it runs. `spec/` was the exception and was broken in a copy, because CLAUDE.md says a file under
  it is never edited here and a reverted edit is still an edit.
- **No new bench line for the duty cycle.** It is upstream's accounting, upstream has it on every
  node, and the thing a bench could add — what our six sends a minute cost a busy channel — is
  measured by the observer that §6's "one packet per send" line already needs.

## Not verified here (and why)

Added to `docs/bench.md` section **6. The mesh client**, tagged **p2-04**; `docs/link.md` §2h is
the page that walks it. Both lines need the **second repeater** and the **observer** section 6
already asks for:

- **The ceiling, and that a correct Walter never meets it.** Seven sends inside a minute, two
  seconds apart: the seventh is `ERR_BUSY` with `STATUS`'s flag bit 1 **clear**, the observer sees
  no packet for it, `client_tx` has risen by six and `cli "sensor get client.refused"` reads 1. A
  minute later the next send goes.
- **The two-second gap.** Two sends as fast as `linkcli.py` can issue them: the second is
  `ERR_BUSY`, nothing leaves, `client.refused` goes up by one. Repeating is unaffected throughout.

And, not on the bench list because no rig is involved:

- **The airtime figures in this report are computed, not measured.** 1.7 s for a 186-byte packet
  and 2.2 s for a 246-byte one come from the LoRa airtime formula with this channel's parameters
  and the 32 preamble symbols `RadioLibWrapper::preambleLengthForSF` sets — the same arithmetic
  RadioLib's `getTimeOnAir` does, which is what upstream's budget uses, but nobody has put a
  counter on a radio. What the percentages are used for is an inequality (22 % against 50 %) with
  room to spare, and the bench's observer is where a real figure would come from.
- **The duty-cycle and queue answers are read from the source, not watched.** No session can see a
  forward wait behind one of our packets; what can be done is to read every line that decides the
  order and the budget, which is the section above and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §7. A bump that
  changes a priority or the budget re-reads those lines — `docs/upstream.md` place 5 now says so.
- **`tools/linkloop/check.sh` cannot show the ceiling.** Each `linkcli.py --pipe` invocation starts
  a fresh `linkloop`, so every send is the first of its boot and the ceiling never fires; a
