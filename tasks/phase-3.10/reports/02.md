# p3.10-02 — The totals, told

01 kept the totals in RAM. This task puts them where the readings already go: in the Cayenne-LPP a
telemetry request is answered with, on the mesh and byte for byte on the box link's `LPP_GET`.
**Nine channels, 6 to 14, chosen here once** — `docs/link.md` §2d is the table, and from this merge
it is a contract. A reader takes two answers and subtracts.

| LPP channel | What | Unit |
|---:|---|---|
| 6 | panel, charge | 0.01 mAh |
| 7 | panel, energy | 0.1 mWh |
| 8 | battery, charge **in** | 0.01 mAh |
| 9 | battery, charge **out** | 0.01 mAh |
| 10 | battery, energy **in** | 0.1 mWh |
| 11 | battery, energy **out** | 0.1 mWh |
| 12 | load, charge | 0.01 mAh |
| 13 | load, energy | 0.1 mWh |
| 14 | the seconds the totals cover | 1 s |

Every one is the four-byte **unsigned** generic sensor (type 100) on its own channel. The units in
the task file are the units built: nothing moved, nothing was renumbered.

### Deliverable 2, the units checked rather than taken

**An hour at 5 mA is hundreds of steps of each, not tens.** 5 mAh is **500** steps of 0.01 mAh. For
energy the lowest rail this board measures is the pack at about 3 V, so 5 mA for an hour is 15 mWh
= **150** steps of 0.1 mWh; on the load at 4 V it is 200 and on the panel at 18 V it is 900. Both
are over the hundred the task asks for, and the two assertions are in
`ATotalIsFourUnsignedBytesAndTheFloorOfItsSteps`. One step finer on either would have cost a year
of the headroom below for nothing; one step coarser would have failed this line.

**What a total above 2^24 steps reads as.** The value on the wire is the step count converted to a
`float` and then truncated into a `uint32_t` (`addField`: `uint32_t v = value * multiplier`, and
the multiplier is 1). A `float` holds every integer to 2^24 = 16 777 216 exactly and above that
only the even ones, so a total **reads as the nearest representable step count** — a multiple of 2
up to 2^25, then 4, and so on. Round-to-nearest is monotone, so **a total never reads lower than
the answer before it**, which is the only promise a reader subtracting two answers needs; the test
asserts that over 4096 consecutive counts at 2^24 and over the same span at 2^30.

**After how long at 20 mA that starts.** Charge: 2^24 steps is 167 772 mAh, which at 20 mA is
8389 hours = **350 days**. Energy, at 20 mA and 4 V (80 mW): 2^24 steps is 1677.7 Wh = 20 972 hours
= **2.4 years**. And the one that does not depend on the current at all: **channel 14 crosses it
after 194 days** of uptime (2^24 seconds), which on a repeater that is not restarted is the first
of the nine to go granular — by one second in 194 days, two in 388.

**And the top, decided.** A total **saturates at 4 294 967 040** steps (`0xFFFFFF00`) and stays
there. The reason it is not `0xFFFFFFFF`: that number is not a `float` — it rounds up to 2^32 — and
converting a `float` of 2^32 or more to a `uint32_t` is **undefined behaviour** in C++, so the
library's own expression would have done whatever the target's conversion instruction happened to
do, which is not a decision. `0xFFFFFF00` is the largest value a `float` holds below 2^32
(16 777 215 × 256), the clamp is applied to the integer step count before the `float` exists, and
`docs/link.md` §2d states it: *it saturates; it never wraps*. In service it is unreachable —
42 949 Ah of charge, 245 years of a 20 mA load — so what will be seen is the rounding above and
never this.

### Deliverable 5, what it costs

**Bytes.** The Cayenne-LPP goes from **36 to 90 bytes**, against a `kMeshLppCap` of 180 — half the
buffer, with 90 bytes free. The reply MeshCore builds around it is a timestamp and those bytes
(`MyMesh.cpp:258-261` at the pin `81dbae59`: `4 + tlen`), encrypted to a 16-byte boundary with a
two-byte MAC and prefixed with two one-byte hashes (`Mesh::createDatagram`,
`Utils::encryptThenMAC`, `CIPHER_BLOCK_SIZE 16` and `CIPHER_MAC_SIZE 2` in `src/MeshCore.h`), then
a header byte and a path-length byte (`Packet::writeTo`):

| | LPP | reply data | encrypted payload | **on air, zero hops** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v0.1.2 | 36 | 40 | 52 | **54 bytes** |
| this | 90 | 94 | 100 | **102 bytes** |

Each byte of returned path adds one byte to the frame.

**Airtime at SF8, 62.5 kHz, CR 4/8** — the Danish channel (`[band_868]` in `platformio.ini`),
computed with the driver's own arithmetic rather than a formula from memory:
`SX126x::calculateTimeOnAir` (RadioLib 7.8.0, on disk), with the **32-symbol preamble** MeshCore
sets for SF ≤ 8 (`RadioLibWrapper::preambleLengthForSF`, called from `CustomSX1262Wrapper`),
explicit header, CRC on, and `ldrOptimize` 0 (never set at this symbol length). Symbol time is
4096 µs; `bitCount = 8·len + 12`; `nSymbol×4 = 177 + 32·ceil(bitCount/32)`:

| | frame | coded blocks | **airtime** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| v0.1.2 | 54 bytes | 14 | **640.0 ms** |
| this | 102 bytes | 26 | **1033.2 ms** |

**+393 ms, +61 %.** At the band's 10 % duty cycle a 1033 ms reply buys 10.3 seconds of silence
after it instead of 6.4; telemetry is asked for hourly, so the cost against the window is 0.03 % of
the hour. Worth saying plainly: this is now the most expensive single packet this firmware sends,
a second requester an hour is still nothing, and an LPP that grew another 54 bytes would be worth a
second look — which is what the 90 free bytes are there to allow and not to invite.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **`p3.10-01` left the link loop unable to read its own INA3221, and this task had to fix the
  harness before it could show anything.** 01 moved the reader onto the device's conversion-ready
  flag; `tools/linkloop/`'s fake bus answers register `0x0F` as zero, so the flag was never raised,
  and the first sweep waited for the two-window fallback — 1690 ms — while one `linkcli` invocation
  lives for about a tenth of a second. `out/verify/linkloop.log` on `main` says `valid 0x00 (none)`
  and *nothing was read* under the heading *the three INA3221 channels, each with its own shunt
  applied*, and `lpp` printed 12 bytes instead of 36. Nothing asserted it, so it stayed green. Two
  changes close it: the fake now **raises and clears the flag off the clock the reader is driven
  on**, as the datasheet says the part does — so the harness exercises the flag path rather than
  the fallback — and a new **`--flowed <seconds>`**, one hour by default, steps the reader through
  that many of the device's own windows on a clock of its own before the pipe opens: the real
  `PowerReader`, the real `PowerTotals`, about 4235 real sweeps. `--flowed 0` is the other case the
  task asks for: a board in its first window, no channel read and the nine left out together.
  `power`, `env` and `lpp` in the log are live again, and `check.sh` now **asserts** the nine lines
  rather than printing them, so the same silence cannot happen twice.
- **`docs/link.md` §2d still said the three channels were "swept once a second"**, which 01 made
  untrue. Corrected in place to one sweep per 845 ms averaging window, found through the flag,
  because the paragraph the totals' section now leans on cannot say something else.
- **`README.md` said 117 bench lines when the page had 118** (01 added one without bumping it).
  With this task's line it is **119**, which is what it now says.
- **The library is asserted, not linked.** The criterion asks that the mirror and the library write
  the same four bytes. CayenneLPP cannot be compiled by `[env:native]`, so the bytes are held
  against hand-computed hex and the library's three *numbers* are held against its own macros in
  the board build (checklist item 2 says which). That is the pattern p1-06 set for this mirror and
  not a new concession, but it is the one claim here that a board build rather than a test
  underwrites.
- **`kMaxLppFields` is now exactly the longest list**, 18, and `telemetryFields` fills it to the
  last slot. A nineteenth field would be silently dropped by `push`, as a tenth was before — so the
  test asserts `telemetryFields(...) == kMaxLppFields` and not only `<=`, which turns a future
  field added without raising the bound into a red test instead of a missing channel.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The floor, not the nearest.** `charge_uc / 36000` truncates, so a total reads at or below the
  truth and never above it, and the error stays bounded by one step rather than accumulating: the
  accumulator keeps its remainder (01), only the view rounds. Tested across a whole step's worth of
  remainders.
- **The ceiling is `0xFFFFFF00` and the clamp is on the integer**, before the `float` exists — see
  deliverable 2. Clamping the `float` instead would have been one comparison the compiler is
  entitled to reason about.
- **The nine are reported even when no channel is valid.** A total is history, not a reading: with
  the B-board unplugged `LPP_GET` answers channels 1 and 6 to 14 and nothing else, because the
  sweeps that are failing are a **gap in channel 14** and that is where a reader is told about
  them. Withdrawing the totals would throw away the hour before the bus died, which is exactly the
  hour somebody wants. `OursNeedTheEnvironmentPermissionAndAnInvalidChannelIsLeftOut` is the case.
- **"A first sweep has counted" is `millisCovered() > 0`**, exposed as `PowerTotals::hasCounted()`.
  A counted sweep always covers at least a millisecond and a gap covers none, so the two cannot be
  confused and no second flag had to be kept.
- **One function copies the accumulator into the snapshot** — `fillTotals()` — rather than the
  snapshot carrying a `PowerTotals`. A snapshot has no business being able to `note()` a sweep, and
  `BoardPower.cpp` and `tools/linkloop/` cannot now copy it two different ways.
- **The mirror's four-byte mask is written out** (`0xFFFFFFFFu`) instead of `(1u << 32) - 1`, which
  is undefined. The library's own expression never meets a four-byte *signed* type so it never
  evaluates it; ours would not either, and now it cannot.
- **`linkcli.py` reads a total's unit off its channel**, not its type, because nine channels share
  type 100. `LPP_TOTALS` beside `LPP_CHANNEL_NAMES` is that table, and `print_lpp` is now
  size-aware so a four-byte field is read as four bytes.
- **`--flowed` defaults to one hour** rather than to zero. That harness's whole purpose is to be a
  board, and a board that has been up an hour is the interesting one; the first-window case is one
  flag away and `check.sh` walks both. The cost is that `status`' uptime (a few milliseconds) and
  channel 14 (3599 s) disagree inside that program, which its header comment says.

## Not verified here (and why)

The phase says it: *Not on the board. The radio is in the unit. No session opens its port*
(`tasks/phase-3.10/README.md`). No port was opened and no walk was run — `bench walk 5` is the two
threshold lines (`docs/bench.md` §0b) and this task's new line is a person's in any case.

- **`docs/bench.md` §5, line 5.2a — two answers an hour apart, and the difference is what the load
  really drew.** The one thing a host test cannot be: a difference needs two answers and an hour
  between them. `linkcli.py lpp`, then the same an hour later, with the load rail held at something
  steady a DMM says the size of. Channel 12's difference ÷ 100 is the milliamp-hours the load drew;
  it must match current × hours inside channel 3's 0.8 mA LSB and the few percent §5's DMM line
  allows. **Channel 14's difference must be 3600 ± a few seconds** — less is the sweeps that were a
  gap, and that shortfall is the honest size of what the other eight do not cover. Then
  `cli "reboot 0"` and read `lpp` again: every total small and **channel 14 lower than before**,
  which is the restart a reader is meant to see and discard its interval over. Reading channel 4 at
  the same two moments gives the Walter's own 110–170 mA, which is the number this pile exists to
  stop meshmaster.dk charting as the hour.
- **The mesh half of the equality.** That `LPP_GET` and a mesh telemetry reply are the same bytes
  is a native test over one field list, and it is the field list and the encoder that are tested,
  not a radio: **no phone has ever read this node's telemetry** (`docs/bench.md` §5, line 5.2 —
  p1-06's, and still open). Channels 6 to 14 arrive in a phone's view as *Channel 6
  genericSensor* and so on — nine numbers that only grow, which is them working until
  meshmaster.dk's own pile names them (`tasks/phase-3.10/README.md`, *Afterwards*).
- **The airtime above is arithmetic, not a measurement.** It is the driver's own
  `calculateTimeOnAir` evaluated by hand at this band's parameters, with the preamble MeshCore
  sets. A spectrum analyser, or the radio's own `getTimeOnAir` read on a board, would be the
  measurement — and 1033 ms of air per telemetry reply is the number to hold a duty-cycle budget
  against if one is ever walked.
