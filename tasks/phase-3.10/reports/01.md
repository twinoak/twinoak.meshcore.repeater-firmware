# p3.10-01 — What flowed

The reader keeps what it sees. `src/common/power/PowerTotals.{h,cpp}` adds every one of the
INA3221's averaging windows up — charge and energy for the panel, the load and the battery **by
direction** — and says how many milliseconds those totals cover. Nothing goes on the wire here;
02 does that.

Two things came out of reading the device rather than the comment above it.

**The averaging field was being described wrong.** `Ina3221.h` said `AVG = 0b100` is 64 samples
and that one sample set therefore takes "64 x 6 x 1.1 ms ~ 422 ms", and the task file repeats it
("the average of the last 0.42 seconds", "the device averages 0.42 s of each second"). The field's
table is 1, 4, 16, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024 for `0b000` to `0b111`, so `0b011` is 64 and **`0b100`
is 128** — read off disk in the pinned Adafruit INA3221 Library 1.0.1
(`INA3221_AVG_128_SAMPLES = 0b100`, beside `INA3221_CONVTIME_1MS = 0b100`, "1.1ms exact"), which
is the INA3221's own datasheet table. The configuration word is **unchanged** (`0x7927`): this
board has been averaging 128 samples since p1-06, one window is 128 × 3 × 2 × 1.1 ms = **844.8 ms**,
and 128 is inside the 64–256 `CURRENT-SENSE.md` §6.7 asks for. Only the arithmetic in the comment
was wrong — and with it the premise that a reading covers 42 % of its second. It covered 84.5 %.

**The device now says when a reading is due.** A sweep begins when the *conversion-ready flag*
(bit 0 of the Mask/Enable register, `0x0F`) says a window has finished, not when a timer of ours
says a second has passed; reading that register is what clears the flag. In continuous mode the
windows tile time end to end, so one reading per window is every reading there is: no window is
read twice, none is skipped, and **the sum of the intervals is the whole of the time the device
was answering**. A free-running 1000 ms sweep against an 845 ms window could not be that: it
covered 844.8 of every 1000 ms, and 18 % of its intervals quietly skipped a whole window.

## Acceptance checklist

- [x] **A steady 100 mA on the load for a simulated hour is 100 mAh to within the rounding you
  state, and the seconds covered are 3600 (test).**
  `TheTotals.ASteadyHundredMilliampsForAnHourIsAHundredMilliampHours`: 4260 windows of 845 ms and
  one last one of 300 ms, which is 3 600 000 ms to the millisecond. `secondsCovered()` is **3600**,
  the charge is **360 000 000 µC** and `milliAmpHours()` is **100**, both exact; the energy at
  4000 mV is 1 440 000 000 µJ and **400 mWh**, also exact. **The rounding:** the two accumulators
  are exact to the millisecond — charge is `mA × ms` (a microcoulomb) and energy is
  `mV × mA × ms` (a nanojoule) carried into microjoules with the remainder kept — and the only
  rounding is the floor in the two convenience accessors, at most 1 mAh and 1 mWh short of the
  truth. What the device's own quantisation costs is below, under the burst.
- [x] **The battery at +500 mA for ten minutes and −50 mA for fifty: the two charges and the two
  energies are each right, and neither direction has the other's (test).**
  `TheTotals.TheBatteryKeepsItsTwoDirectionsApart`, at 4100 mV charging and 3900 mV draining:
  in **300 000 000 µC** (83 mAh) and **1 230 000 000 µJ** (341 mWh), out **150 000 000 µC**
  (41 mAh) and **585 000 000 µJ** (162 mWh), each the hand-computed product; the panel and the
  load, which read 0 mA throughout, have nothing at all.
- [x] **A burst inside one sweep interval is counted for that interval and no other (test).**
  `TheTotals.ABurstIsCountedForItsOwnIntervalAndNoOther`: 100 mA, then 1500 mA, then 100 mA, a
  window each, and the running total after each is `100×845`, `1600×845`, `1700×845` µC.
- [x] **A failed channel, the back-off and a late sweep each leave a gap: the totals do not move
  and the seconds covered do not count it (tests).** Four, three of them through the real reader:
  `TheTotals.AChannelThatDidNotReadIsAGapForAllThree` (one invalid channel, and the two that did
  read add nothing either — a sweep counts for all three or for none);
  `TheReader.AChannelThatFailsGoesInvalidAndComesBack` (the same thing driven off the bus, and
  the window after it is one window and not two); `TheReader.ADeviceThatIsNotThereIsLeftAloneAndFoundWhenItComes`
  (the five-second back-off is in no total, because `configure()` re-anchors the totals' clock);
  `TheTotals.ALateSweepIsAGapAndTheOneAtTheBoundIsNot` (the bound itself counts, one millisecond
  past it does not, and the gap is that interval and not the next one as well). The stated bound
  is **`kMaxIntervalMs` = one and a half windows = 1267 ms**: normal is 845 to 870 ms, and two
  windows — 1690 ms — would mean the device finished one that nobody read, so the average in its
  registers covers half the interval and not the whole of it.
- [x] **A year at full scale on every channel overflows nothing (test, by arithmetic on the
  types).** `TheTotals.AYearAtFullScaleOverflowsNothing`: four `static_assert`s on the exact
  expressions the accumulators compute, at this board's full scale (1638, 8190 and 3276 mA) and
  the bus register's own (4095 × 8 = 32 760 mV). A year is **2.58e14 µC** (71 000 years of room in
  a `uint64`) and **8.46e15 µJ** (2 100 years); one interval's own nanojoule product is 3.4e11, far
  inside the type. Nanojoules as the accumulator would have been 8.5e18 a year — two years of
  room — which is why `add()` carries a remainder into microjoules instead, and
  `TheTotals.NothingBelowAMicrojouleIsLost` is that remainder (999 steps of 1 nJ are still 0 µJ;
  the thousandth is 1 µJ, and no step was lost). The arithmetic is then run for 100 000 steps at
  full scale and held to the hand-computed product.
- [x] **The battery thresholds' tests pass unchanged, or the report says which changed and why.**
  **Unchanged**, all six of them, byte for byte: `BatteryWatch` and its cache contract did not
  move. One test was *added* beside them — `TheTotals.TheThresholdsSeeWhatTheySaw` — which drives
  the watch from the real reader on the new schedule: every window's bus voltage reaches it in
  order, `lastMilliVolts()` is the cache's own number, and a pack at 3440 mV is `kBatteryLow`
  after the minute, while the totals of the same windows are exact.
- [x] **No request handler performs I2C (the existing rail).**
  `Rails.AlsoKept_NoRequestHandlerCanReachAnI2cBus` passes. The two new files name no bus and no
  driver; the only I2C calls are still inside `PowerReader::loop()` and `EnvReader::loop()`, and

  at most one channel (two register reads) *or* one flag poll (one register read), never both.

### Deliverable 4, the two numbers it asks for

**How much of the time a total really covers.** With the flag gate: **all of it.** Every
microsecond between two consecutive good sweeps is in exactly one interval, the intervals are
measured read to read (`int32_t(now - last)`, never an assumed second), and each one is covered by
exactly one of the device's 844.8 ms windows, which tile time. The residue is *phase*, not
coverage: the reader notices the flag up to `kReadyPollMs` = 25 ms after it goes up, so a given
window's average is spread over an interval shifted by at most 25 ms — 3 % of one sample, and it
does not accumulate, because consecutive intervals share their boundaries and their sum is the
elapsed time exactly. Before this task the figure was **84.5 %** (844.8 ms of every 1000 ms), and
worse than the number suggests: 18 % of the intervals contained two completed windows, so one
window's average was silently taken for 1000 ms of charge.

**A 100 ms burst of 1.5 A.** Its own charge is 0.15 C = **41.7 µAh** (0.0417 mAh) and, at a 3.7 V
pack, 0.555 J = 0.154 mWh.

- *Best case, now:* the burst falls inside one window; the device's hardware average carries all
  of it (the window reads 177.6 mA above idle on channel 3), and the hour's total gains the full
  41.7 µAh **±3 %** from the interval-against-window mismatch and **±0.2 %** from channel 3's
  0.8 mA LSB. A burst that *straddles* two windows is also exact, because both windows are read.
- *Worst case, now:* the burst lies in a window whose sweep was a gap — a channel that did not
  answer, the back-off, a starved loop, or the fallback below. Then all 41.7 µAh of it is lost,
  and so is the rest of that window: 0.0417 mAh against a 30 mAh hour is **0.14 %** of the hour,
  and the lost window itself is 0.023 % of it.
- *For comparison, before this task:* the same burst was worth anywhere from **0** (its window was
  one of the 18 % never read) to **+18 %** (its window's average stretched over a 1000 ms
  interval).

**What discarding a negative reading costs on the panel and the load, at one step of noise.**
Symmetric noise around zero becomes a one-sided bias of at most one *rounded* LSB. On channel 1
one LSB is 0.4 mA, which `currentMilliAmps` rounds to **0 mA**, so one step of noise on the panel
adds exactly nothing; two steps (0.8 mA) would round to 1 mA and cost at most 1 mAh an hour,
24 mAh a day. On channel 3 one LSB is 0.8 mA → **1 mA**, so a channel truly at zero dithering by
one step would cost up to 1 mAh an hour, 24 mAh a day — but the load never is at zero (20–60 mA
idle for the two radios), so in practice it costs nothing there either, and the panel at night is
where the bias could live and is the channel whose LSB rounds away. The device's own input offset
is tens of microvolts (`CURRENT-SENSE.md` §3.5), which is ≤0.5 mA on channel 1's 100 mΩ and
≤0.8 mA on channel 3's 50 mΩ — the same order. The battery channel keeps both signs and has no
such bias.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The task's "0.42 seconds" is 0.845 seconds**, and "the device averages 0.42 s of each second"
  is 84.5 % of each second, not 42 %. The cause is the comment in `Ina3221.h` that the task file
  was written from: `AVG = 0b100` is 128 samples, not 64. Fixed where it lives, with the on-disk
  source cited; the configuration word is byte for byte what it was, so no image behaves
  differently for this correction alone. Nothing else in the task changes because of it — the
  complaint it rests on (a reading is a moment, and the only moment anybody asks is the worst one)
  is true of an 845 ms window exactly as of a 422 ms one.
- **`kSweepIntervalMs` is gone.** The schedule is the device's flag, with `kReadyPollFromMs` (740 ms,
  an eighth of a window early, so a device running up to 12 % fast cannot finish a second window
  unnoticed) and `kReadyPollMs` (25 ms). I2C goes from 6 transactions per 1000 ms to 6 + 1 to 5
  per 845 ms — about 11 a second against 6, each ~0.5 ms at 100 kHz, so ~0.6 % of the loop.
- **The first reading after boot now arrives one window (845 ms) after the configuration write**,
  where before `begin()` read the registers at once. Those registers hold no completed average at
  that moment — zeros on a cold power-up — so this is a correction and not a loss: `status` and
  upstream's boot-voltage check read 0 mV in that first second either way, which is what
  `docs/bench.md` §5's boot-voltage line already records.
- **Six reader tests changed shape** (they drive the fake through the flag now) and one changed
  name — `AHealthyDeviceIsConfiguredOnceAndSweptOnceASecond` →
  `…AndSweptOncePerAveragingWindow`. Four reader tests are new. No threshold, telemetry, link-handler
  or preferences test changed.
- **A failed read of the Mask/Enable register drops the device** into the five-second back-off,
  where a failed read of one *channel* register is only a missing reading. It is the register that
  is the device itself rather than a range of it, and `configure()` already treats the
  manufacturer ID that way. `TheReader.ABusThatWillNotSayWhetherItHasConvertedIsTheDeviceGoingAway`.
- **`tools/bench/test_walk.py` has one number changed**: §5 now has seven lines that need a
  person, not six, because this task added one to the page. That is the test of the tool holding
  the page, working as intended.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The accumulators' units.** Charge in **microcoulomb** (`mA × ms`) and energy in **microjoule**
  with a nanojoule remainder carried, because nanojoules alone give only two years of headroom at
  full scale and the task asks for years. `milliAmpHours()` and `milliWattHours()` are floors over
  the same divisor (3 600 000). What the wire carries is 02's to choose; both the raw and the
  rounded forms are exposed so that it can.
- **`kMaxIntervalMs` = one and a half windows (1267 ms)** as the "later than a bound you choose"
  bound: above the honest 845–870 ms and below the 1690 ms that means a window was missed.
- **A gap re-anchors the clock but keeps the totals.** `anchor()` moves where the next interval is
  measured from and never clears a total (`TheTotals.AnAnchorMovesTheClockAndClearsNoTotal`), which
  is what "zero at start, and only then" has to mean when a device can be reconfigured.
- **A fallback, after two windows of silence from the flag** (`kReadyWaitMaxMs`). That a real
  INA3221 raises that flag in continuous mode is read out of the datasheet and the pinned
  Adafruit library's register map, and **no test on a machine with no INA3221 on it can prove it**.
  Without a fallback, a part that did not raise it would answer nothing for ever — no `POWER_GET`,
  no battery voltage, no thresholds — on a unit that is on a roof and reachable only by telemetry.
  So the reader reads the registers anyway after 1690 ms, which is exactly what it did before this
  task and is a true reading of the last window the device finished. Such a sweep is past
  `kMaxIntervalMs` and is therefore **a gap**: the firmware keeps answering and the totals say
  honestly that they do not cover that time. `sweepsWithoutTheFlag()` counts them — 0 on a healthy
  part — and `TheReader.APartThatNeverRaisesTheFlagIsStillRead` is the case.
- **The totals live inside `PowerReader`** (composition, `reader.totals()`), because the reader is
  the only thing that knows when a window ended and whether all three channels answered. The class
  itself is driven directly by its own tests, so the arithmetic is held without a bus.
- **A window is attributed to the instant the flag was seen**, not to the instant the last of its
  three channels came off the bus: the three reads are three passes of `loop()` apart and that is
  our latency, not the device's.

## New libraries (with the one-line justification)

None. The Adafruit INA3221 Library is already in `[libs].sensors` (upstream's sensor helpers
compile it whether a part is fitted or not) and is **read, not called**: it is the on-disk copy of
the datasheet's register map and averaging table that `Ina3221.h` now cites. `test/test_rails`
still fails any board environment that turns upstream's own INA3221 support on.

## Not verified here (and why)

The phase says it: *Not on the board. The radio is in the unit. No session opens its port*
(`tasks/phase-3.10/README.md`). No port was opened and `bench walk 5` was not run — the one new
line is a person's in any case (`docs/bench.md` §0b: §5's walk is the two threshold lines, and
this one needs the boards, the DMM and the link).

- **`docs/bench.md` §5, line 5.1a — the readings keep arriving, on the device's own schedule.**
  The one claim no host test can reach: that a real INA3221 raises the conversion-ready flag once
  per averaging window in continuous mode. `cli "reboot 0"`, then `linkcli.py power` as soon as
  the link is back and four more times a few seconds apart: `valid 7` every time, the three
  numbers alive and agreeing with §5's DMM line, and the **first** reading inside about a second
  of the restart. Inside a second is the flag; nearer two seconds is `kReadyWaitMaxMs` carrying it,
  which is not a fault of the board but a finding for this phase's follow-ups. `cli "status"`
  carries a battery voltage from the same cache while the rig is up.
- **The totals themselves cannot be read from the unit yet.** Nothing in this task puts them on a
  wire — that is 02, on LPP channels 6 to 14, and its own bench line will be the one that shows
  the difference between two answers against a known load. Until then the only way to see these
  numbers on hardware is the line above, which shows that the windows they are built from are
  arriving.
