# p3.1-01 — the-fast-sweep

NoiseScope has a fourth way of looking at the spectrum: **`scope`**, the sweep
run *inside* the SX1262. A 167-word program for the radio's own core — MeshCore
issue [#3578](https://github.com/meshcore-dev/MeshCore/issues/3578), vendored —
retunes the receiver after every RSSI update and leaves one level per point in
the data buffer, so 250 points cost one SPI command and tens of milliseconds
where the MCU-stepped `sweep` costs seconds. Fast enough for a live waterfall,
which is what the plotter now draws.

What differs from the issue is the three things the task asked for and one more
that fell out of them:

* **the span is the operator's**, up to the whole 150–960 MHz the radio tunes,
  cut into segments of at most 32 MHz and 250 points, **each calibrated for its
  own range** — the issue caps one sweep at 32 MHz for exactly that reason;
* **a frame goes to the port its command came in on** and sized for it: USB CDC
  gets every sweep, the Walter's 115200 UART gets the interval that port can
  carry, and the `ack` says which;
* **a frame is one NDJSON line** like every other, with the levels base64'd, so
  the Walter's verbatim forwarding and every reader go on working;
* and the plugin is behind a **ROM gate** — it is made for one ROM string, and
  on any other chip `scope` uploads nothing and says so.

`sweep`, `hist`, `dwell`, `cad`, `stat` and `boot` are untouched. `info` has one
new field, `rom`.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

**None from `spec/`** — the scanner is not a subject of `spec/SYSTEM.md` beyond
S-11 (a family of roles) and S-10 (local first), and both hold.

Three things differ from the task's text, each smaller than it sounds:

1. **The vendored class is a fifth file, and it is edited in two places.** The
   task asked for four files; `LICENSE.txt` (the fork's own `license.txt` at
   `f40f469d`) is beside them because MIT asks for the notice to travel with
   the copy. The two edits to `SX126xSpectrumScanner.h` are marked `TwinOak`
   where they stand and are in *Decisions* below.
2. **`kScopeMaxSegments` is not a refusal.** The task's plan has the span cut
   into segments; an early draft refused a plan needing more than 64 of them,
   which would have made *lowering* `kScopeSegmentMhz` at the bench (which the
   task explicitly allows) start refusing wide scopes. There is no such refusal
   now: a segment carries at least one point, so a plan never has more segments
   than points, and the constant records that identity. The worst case at
   32 MHz is 26 segments, which the test finds by walking every point count the
   command takes rather than by being told.
3. **A segment's calibration range is what it *measures***, not its nominal
   bins: from half a receiver bandwidth below its first point's centre to half
   above its last. The two are the same thing when a point is about as wide as
   the filter, and they differ sharply when they are not — `scope 150 960 16`
   is one point per segment with a 50 MHz step, and calibrating 50 MHz for a
   measurement of one frequency would be exactly the mistake the segments
   exist to avoid. This was found by the test that walks every point count
   (the first draft produced segments 50 MHz wide against a 32 MHz bound).

## Decisions taken without asking

**The two defaults of `scope` are circular, and this is the order.** The task
gives `points` "enough for no gaps at the RBW" and `rbw_khz` "the narrowest
bandwidth ≥ 1.1 × step", each defined in terms of the other, and says nothing
about what `scope 863 870` alone means. Decided: a **bandwidth asked for is a
resolution asked for**, so the point count follows it (`scope 150 960 0 467` →
1735 points, the task's own number); with **neither** given, `points` is **250**
— one plugin call, the number the fork's timings are quoted for — and the
bandwidth follows the step. The alternative, 2048 points by default, would make
`scope 863 870` a 1.5 Hz waterfall, and the task's own bench line expects "tens
of sweeps a second" from exactly that command.

**The two edits to the vendored scanner class** (`TwinOak (1)` and `(2)` in the
file, and `docs/upstream.md` place 8 now names it for a RadioLib bump):

1. *RadioLib 7.8.0 is not the RadioLib the fork builds against.* MeshCore's
   build defines `RADIOLIB_GODMODE=1` (its `platformio.ini` line 28), which
   makes `SX126x::mod`, `readRegister()`, `setRx()` and `setBufferBaseAddress()`
   public. 7.8.0 keeps all four protected without it (`SX126x.h`:867-892), and
   `[env:scanner_rak4630_single]` deliberately defines neither GODMODE nor
   `RADIOLIB_LOW_LEVEL` — `ScannerHeaders.h` says why (upstream's `#warning`
   is the one thing `#pragma GCC system_header` does not silence, and this
   environment has no `-Wno-error=cpp`). So the file gained a six-line subclass
   of `SX1262` that hands those four back out, and `_radio` is one of those.
   **Not one byte of what the class sends the chip changed**: the parameter
   bytes, the latency table, the poll timing and the image calibration at the
   span's centre are the fork's to the digit.
2. *Boosted gain is the scanner's `boost` setting*, as the task asked, where
   the fork hard-codes `true`. `begin()` takes it as an argument.

**The current limit is set after `begin()`, not inside it.** The vendored
`begin()` does not set one; `Radio.cpp` calls the scanner's own `applyRxPath()`
straight afterwards, so a `scope` reading goes through the same 140 mA limit,
the same DIO2 RF switch and the same gain as every other reading this firmware
takes — which is the whole reason they are comparable with what the repeater
hears.

**The segment's calibration is applied after the frequency is set, not before.**
`SX1262::setFrequency()` recalibrates by itself on a move of 20 MHz or more
(`RADIOLIB_SX126X_CAL_IMG_FREQ_TRIG_MHZ`), and it would calibrate for one of
Semtech's five bands or ±4 MHz around the centre. `armSegment()` therefore sets
the centre first — the same value `setSweep()` computes, so `setSweep`'s own
call is a move of zero and calibrates nothing — then calls
`calibrateImageRejection(lo, hi)` in standby, where the CalibrateImage command
belongs. The vendored class needed no sixth argument for it.

**One segment per pass of `loop()`.** A plugin call blocks for its own duration
and feeds nothing; running the *whole* span inside one `service()` would be up
to 0.9 s with nothing drained and nothing fed. One segment a pass keeps the
worst blocking at ~86 ms (250 points at the narrowest filter, well inside
RadioLib's 1000 ms wait for BUSY — `Module.h`:224 — and the 8 s watchdog), and
both bounds are computed by the plan and asserted by a test over every legal
request.

**`scope` requires both ends of its span.** `sweep 865` is the default sweep
(the band's), and that rule is kept for `sweep`; `scope` has no default span,
because the span is the whole point of the mode, so `scope 865` is refused with
a usage line.

**`spectrum.asm` is excluded from `build_src_filter`.** It is a program for the
*radio's* core; without that line PlatformIO hands it to `arm-none-eabi-as`,
which has opinions about `move r3, [P_NUM]`. One line in `platformio.ini` with
a comment saying so.

**The plotter's `--range` means dB over the baseline when `--baseline` is
given**, since that is what is being drawn. Said in `--help`.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session has a radio, so **nothing below has been seen happen** — only
planned, framed and compiled. All six are in `docs/bench.md` §9 under *The fast
sweep*, in the order to walk them, and the first one gates the rest:

1. **The radio's ROM.** `info` → `rom`. If it is not `SX1261 V2D 2D02`, stop:
   the plugin is a program for that core and `scope` will refuse. Nothing in
   this repository can tell what the RAK4630's stamp actually says.
2. **`scope 863 870` over USB with the plotter**: frames at tens of sweeps a
   second, the home channel's traffic as bursts in the waterfall, and `sps`
   agreeing with the fork's numbers (~21 ms for 250 points at 19.5 kHz). The
   arithmetic that predicts that is tested; that the part behaves that way is
   the author's measurement and not ours.
3. **The dummy-load baseline**, `scope 150 960 2048` into a file. The radio's
   own spurs and the matching network's shape across the tuning range: unknown
   until a part is on a bench, and every later reading is worth more with it.
4. **The image check** — the one that could change a constant. A generator at a
   known frequency through an attenuator, a wide `scope` around it, and one
   peak where it is. If a ghost shows, `kScopeSegmentMhz` comes down until it
   does not, and the number is written down. 32 MHz is the fork's figure for
   *its* hardware; ours is the same chip but not the same front end.
5. **The site through the cavity filter**, `scope 791 960` with the baseline
   subtracted.
6. **The same `scope` from the Walter UART**: the `ack`'s interval is several
   hundred milliseconds and not 0, frames arrive at about that rate, `stat`
   keeps coming on **both** ports, and ten minutes pass with no watchdog reset.
   The floor is computed and tested; that 115200 baud and this loop behave as
   the arithmetic says needs the two consoles.

Also not verified here, and not verifiable anywhere in this repository: that
`uploadPatch` of a plugin written for the ROM's own instruction set does what
its author says on a part that is not his. The ROM gate and the tag check
before every sweep are what stand between that and a chip running whatever is
in its RAM.
