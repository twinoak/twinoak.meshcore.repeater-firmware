# p3.5-05 — The Danish standard

**A fresh unit now carries the whole of the Danish standard that is not per site, and one of its
settings is different on every unit by design.** The first-boot profile is **six** lines: the two
it had, plus `set agc.reset.interval 4`, `set loop.detect moderate`, `set flood.max.unscoped 15`
— which comes off the profile's *deliberately not here* list — and `set flood.advert.interval <h>`,
where `h = 60 + (k mod 26)` and `k` is the first byte of the node's own public key, so that units
flashed in one evening do not flood their adverts in step for as long as they run.

**The board walked it.** `bench walk 10` on the RAK4630: an `erase`, a first boot, and a console
that answered `> 4`, `> moderate`, `> 15` and **`> 65`** — where `0x87` is the first byte of the key
that boot made, `135 mod 26 = 5`, and `60 + 5 = 65`. `sensor get firstboot.state` said **`done`**,
so upstream's own interpreter accepted every one of the six lines, the derived one included; a
`reboot` left all four where they were and `firstboot.state` at `never`. **26 ok, 0 differs.**

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The profile is six lines, not five.** The task's deliverables are "three static lines" plus "one
  line built from the key"; the table already had two, so the shipped count is **six**. Every place
  that said *two* now says six: `test_firstboot`, `test_rails` rule 4, `docs/provisioning.md`,
  `docs/bench.md` §10, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §4a, and the console's own
  `First boot: the profile ran 6 of 6 lines`.
- **`docs/bench.md` §10 grew two bullets rather than growing two already-ticked ones.** The task says
  "walk 10's steps grow those four `get`s", and the four `get`s *are* steps of §10 — but 10.1 and
  10.2 are already ticked `(bench, 2026-10-22, 9fb721b)`, and `--tick` leaves a ticked line alone
  (*already ticked*), so four new checks hidden inside them could never be ticked by the walk that
  made them. They are **10.1a** and **10.2a**, *And the Danish standard reached it* and *And the
  Danish standard was saved with it*, each with its own `→ ______` and its own task tag, and both
  came back **ok** and are ticked. The suffixed keys are deliberate: 10.3 to 10.7 keep the numbers
  that p3-03's, p3.3-01's and p3.4-02's reports already cite.
- **`docs/bench.md` 10.2's "both lines of it" is now "every line of it"**, which is the only word
  changed inside an already-ticked bullet: the claim was about the profile's work surviving a
  restart and that claim is unchanged; "both" had become wrong arithmetic. The anchor the walk holds
  the page by is the bullet's bold opening and was not touched.
- **`tools/linkloop/`'s miniature of upstream's `set` answered a bare `OK` for all four keys**, and
  three of them now go through it on every `--first-boot` run. Each answers what the pinned
  `CommonCLI.cpp` answers instead — `agc.reset.interval` the `OK - interval rounded to <4n>` of its
  seconds/4 rounding, `loop.detect` its four words or
  `Error, must be: off, minimal, moderate, or strict`, `flood.max.unscoped` its `Error, max 64`,
  `flood.advert.interval` its `Error: interval range is 3-168 hours` — because what that loop exists
  to watch is the profile's **reply rule**, and a bare `OK` would have hidden the one line of the six
  whose success is not an `OK`. `check.sh` now sends those three refusals and greps for upstream's
  own wording.

## Decisions taken without asking

1. **`k` is the key's first byte.** The task says "a rule this task defines … (for example, its first
   byte)", so the example is taken, for three reasons: upstream already treats that byte as the
   node's identity on the air (`Identity::copyHashTo`'s "hash is just prefix of pub_key"), it is the
   byte `main.cpp` refuses `0x00` and `0xFF` for, and it is the byte a person can read off the
   `Repeater ID:` line and check the hour against by hand — which is exactly what `bench walk 10`
   does. A hash of the whole key would spread no better (the key is random) and could not be checked
   without a tool.
2. **`60 + (k mod 26)`, and `60`/`26` are two named constants** (`kFloodAdvertHoursMin`,
   `kFloodAdvertHoursSpan`) in the header, because three other places need the same two numbers:
   the native tests, `docs/provisioning.md`, and `tools/bench/walk.py`, which **reads them out of
   `Profile.h`** rather than carrying them, the way it reads the band out of `platformio.ini`.
3. **The runner's growth is one parameter and one field.** `runProfile` takes a `const NodeFacts&`
   (the key and its length — nothing else of the node), and a `ProfileLine` may carry a `build`
   function instead of being text. A row that does keeps its `command` as the text a person reads,
   `set flood.advert.interval <h>`, and that text is what a refusal prints — so the console can
   still name the first refused line without printing a value of this node. The alternatives were a
   `printf` template in the table (a footgun in a table meant to be edited without reading C++) and
   a second table of generated lines (two tables where the design's whole point is one).
4. **The key exists before the profile runs, so no order moved.** `src/roles/repeater/main.cpp`
   loads `_main` from the identity store, or draws a new identity and saves it, about a dozen lines
   above the `twinoak::firstBoot()` call, and prints it as `Repeater ID:`. `firstBoot()` therefore
   reads `mesh.self_id.pub_key` (`mesh::Mesh`'s own public member, `src/Mesh.h:180` at the pin) and
   the board side gained no parameter. The task asked for "the order moves, or this line runs once
   the key exists; say which": neither was needed.
5. **A line that cannot be built is refused, not skipped and not fatal.** It is counted like any
   other refusal, so `sensor get firstboot.state` says `partial: 1 refused` and the console names
   the line — the same contract p3-03 chose for a line upstream does not take.
6. **`tools/linkloop/` uses a fixed 32-byte key** whose first byte is 13, so the derived line behind
   the pipe is always `set flood.advert.interval 73`. A host program has no identity store; a fixed
   key makes the loop's output reproducible and `check.sh`'s `> done` still proves the derived line
   was built and accepted.
7. **The fake board applies the profile on a first boot and upstream's defaults on an `erase`**
   (`tools/bench/fakes.py`), with the `60 + (k mod 26)` rule mirrored in Python — the way
   `restart()`/`boot()` already mirror the clock keeper (p3.5-04). So the walk's computed hour is
   checked against a board that derived it independently, and `test_walk.py` has three new cases
   where they disagree: a board that kept upstream's four defaults (both lines *differ*, with a
   `[05]` follow-up), a board that answers upstream's 47 while the other three took (that line
   alone differs), and a profile with a static line removed (that step is *skipped*, not a failure,
   because the table is Thomas's to change).
8. **The three delays stay out, and the guest password stays out.** `rxdelay`, `txdelay` and
   `direct.txdelay` are the neighbours count the task and `docs/provisioning.md` say they are;
   `guest.password hello` is still `[Thomas]` in phase 3's follow-ups and could not be a profile
   line anyway (S-13, and a test greps for it).

## Not verified here (and why)

Four lines of `docs/bench.md` §10 are still open after this walk, and none of them is this task's
to close:

- **10.1's own two `First boot:` console lines** (`needs a person`). They go out about a second
  after the reset and only while a host holds the port, and the port is not back before 1.4 s
  (p3.4-02): a terminal already open across the reset button is the only way to read
  `First boot: the profile ran 6 of 6 lines`. What they leave behind is what the walk checks — a new
  key, `firstboot.state` → `done`, and the six settings. **The `6 of 6` string itself has not been
  printed by a board**; it is `main.cpp`'s own arithmetic over `ran + refused`.
- **10.3, the power cut in the middle** (`needs a person`): `erase`, reset, and the lab supply pulled
  during the first second, three or four times. Nothing to see is no `First boot:` line together
  with `get path.hash.mode` → `0`.
- **10.5, a phone logging in as admin** (`needs a person`): a phone on the mesh — and the password
  this walk set is one nobody has, which is what a bench unit is.
- **10.6, the provisioning over the link** (`skipped`): there is no `dongle_serial` in
  `bench.local.json`, so the same seven lines went in at the USB port instead (10.4, `ok`). With the
  3.3 V USB-UART dongle on the link pins the walk sends them through `linkcli.py provision` and
  checks that the password is four stars.

And one thing no bench can show: **whether 60–85 hours is the right spread.** The rule gives 26
distinct periods, which for a batch of ten units means some pair shares a period more often than not
(the birthday arithmetic: about 86 % of such batches). What the spread really buys is not distinct
periods but distinct *phases* — two units on the same 73 hours still advertise whenever each of them
last did, and they walk apart from upstream's single 47, where every unit of a batch flashed in one
evening keeps the same period **and** nearly the same phase for as long as it runs. 60–85 is the
Danish standard's own range as Thomas gave it and not a measurement; what it is worth will show on a
roof, with a companion app and a week.
