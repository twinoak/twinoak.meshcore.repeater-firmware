# p3.5-03 — A watchdog an update gets through

**A scanner is replaced by the repeater in one DFU.** The 8 s hardware watchdog that ended the
first one is at **60 s** now — twice the longest update ever measured on this bench, rounded up to
whole tens — and the 8 s check NoiseScope was designed around is where it belongs: the main loop
leaves a heartbeat, an RTC2 interrupt asks every 125 ms whether one has been left, and eight
seconds of silence is a reset. Both watchdogs are the one `reset` word `wdt`, with a new `wdt`
field saying which half. And `hang` makes the whole thing visible: `docs/bench.md` §9's *The
watchdog is live* — *a session cannot cause one* — is **ticked**, walked on the board.

The measurement is in the tool and not only in this report: every `bench flash` now logs `dfu_s`,
`update_s` and `attempts`, so `out/bench/flash.log` says how long an update took and whether it
took one DFU or two.

## The measurement

Three runs were asked for; there are four, because the `bench restore` that closed the run is the
same measurement and is in the log beside them. Each is a flash of
`out/build/repeater_rak4630_single/firmware.zip` (414 052 bytes of application, 414 871 of
package) onto a board already running the repeater — so the touch comes from an image with **no**
watchdog and what is timed is the update and nothing else, which is what the task asks for
(*where from: the bootloader, where no watchdog runs*).

| # | `at` | `dfu_s` (adafruit-nrfutil) | `update_s` (DFU → the application's port) | DFUs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20:50:03Z | 25.2 | **26.3** | 1 |
| 2 | 20:50:44Z | 25.2 | **26.3** | 1 |
| 3 | 20:51:24Z | 25.2 | **26.3** | 1 |
| 4 | 20:52:22Z (the restore) | 25.2 | **26.3** | 1 |

The four agree to the tenth of a second, which is what a bulk transfer bounded by the device and
not by the host looks like. The `-b 115200` the platform passes is nominal — the bootloader's port
is USB CDC, where the baud setting is a line-coding request nothing throttles — so what sets the
25.2 s is the DFU protocol's round-trips and the nRF52840's own flash writes: 414 871 package bytes
in 25.2 s is about **16.4 kB/s**, and `adafruit-nrfutil`'s progress bar is 809 hashes of about
512 bytes each. The remaining 1.1 s is the activation and the USB re-enumeration
(`4. waiting up to 40 s …`). A larger application would therefore scale this number, which is one
reason the measurement is logged on every flash rather than only here.

So **26.3 × 2 = 52.6 s → 60 s**, the next whole ten. On this part that is `CRV` =
`60 × 32768 = 1 966 080`, far inside the register, and it means the longest a real hang can go
unreset by the hardware half is 60 s where it used to be 8 — which is what the software half at 8 s
is for.

For the record, the other two directions are not what sets the number but are in the log:
scanner → scanner is **10.7 s** and repeater → scanner **8.7 s** (126 048 bytes). The repeater is
the largest image the scanner is replaced by, which is why it is the one the timeout is derived
from.

## How the two halves are built

**The deadline is arithmetic** (`src/roles/scanner/LoopWatch.h`). `beat()` from the loop,
`tick()` from whatever keeps the cadence; `tick()` returns `true` exactly once, when the loop has
not come round inside the deadline. Both touch single words, each word with one writer per state,
which on a Cortex-M4 is a store nothing can tear — so there is no lock and no interrupt disabled
anywhere, and the worst an unlucky interleaving can do is carry one heartbeat into the next window
and cost a tick of lateness.

**The board half is registers and a vector** (`src/roles/scanner/LoopWdt.{h,cpp}`), and four
decisions are in it:

1. **RTC2**, because the question must be asked by something the loop cannot stop and by a
   peripheral this image does not already use. RTC0 is the SoftDevice's, RTC1 is FreeRTOS's tick
   on this core (`cores/nRF5/freertos/portable/CMSIS/nrf52/portmacro_cmsis.h`:
   `portNRF_RTC_REG NRF_RTC1`), and `TIMER2->CC[0]` carries the bootloader's version to the
   application (`cores/nRF5/wiring.c`). RTC2 is claimed by nothing: the core ships **no
   `nrfx_rtc.c`**, so `RTC2_IRQHandler` in our file is the only definition in the image and the
   weak one of `gcc_startup_nrf52840.S` is what it replaces. Nothing under `src/` names an nRF
   timer or RTC either — the RV-3028 on the adapter is an I2C part, not this one.
2. **The TICK event, not a compare.** `PRESCALER 4095` makes the counter increment at 8 Hz, so
   TICK is an interrupt every 125 ms with no compare register to re-arm and no *a compare set one
   tick ahead may be missed* to reason about. 8000 / 125 = 64 ticks exactly.
3. **The marker is `.noinit` RAM, not `GPREGRET`** — the task's own instruction, and the reason is
   that `GPREGRET` is the Adafruit bootloader's mailbox, which this firmware already writes its
   two `dfu` magic words into.
4. **The reset is `NVIC_SystemReset()`**, so `RESETREAS` says `SREQ`; the marker is what makes
   `boot` say `wdt` all the same, and `captureResetReason()` reads and clears it before
   `loopWdtBegin()`.

**Where `.noinit` lands is placed and asserted, not assumed** (`src/roles/scanner/loopwdt.ld`,
added to the link by `tools/pio/loopwdt.py`). This core's linker script has no `.noinit` section,
and an orphan one could have been placed inside the range `Reset_Handler` copies `.data` into — in
which case the marker would have read the same value after every reset and the whole mechanism
would have been silently dead. So the fragment is one `INSERT AFTER .bss` block, the same
mechanism the core's own `ldscript.ld` uses for `.svc_data` and `.fs_data`, and it is **after
`.bss`** (outside both the copy and the zero ranges), **before `.heap`** (so `__HeapBase` is above
it and malloc cannot hand it out) and **`(NOLOAD)`** (no flash cost, no RAM address in
`firmware.bin`). The map of this build:

    __bss_end__   0x2000fa30
    .noinit       0x2000fa30  0x4   <- src/roles/scanner/LoopWdt.cpp.o
    __HeapBase    0x2000fa34

and `readelf -S` says `NOBITS`, with `firmware.bin` the size the section table says. Two `ASSERT`s
in the fragment hold both bounds at link time, and they were checked by breaking them: with
`INSERT AFTER .data` instead, the link fails with *the loop watchdog's marker is inside .bss or
.data: Reset_Handler would clear it on every boot*.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The `boot` field that tells the two watchdogs apart is `wdt`, and it is always present.** The
  task leaves the field's existence open (*if the two are told apart, it is in a field §5's NDJSON
  allows to be added*). It is worth having — a reader who sees `wdt` wants to know whether the
  loop check caught it or whether the part was so far gone that the hardware one had to — and it
  is spelled like `info`'s `rom`: empty rather than absent, because *a reader that looks for it
  finds a field* (`src/roles/scanner/Lines.cpp`, and that comment is upstream of this task).
  `reset` keeps its five words exactly as `docs/noisescope.md` §5 enumerates them.
- **Four measurements, not three.** The fourth is the `bench restore` that closed the run; it is
  the same flash, it was going to happen anyway, and leaving it out of the table would have been a
  choice about which lines of the log to show.
- **`tools/bench/flash.py` grew three fields, which the task did not ask for.** It asks for the
  times to be measured *with `tools/bench`* and recorded in the report. A session timing a
  subprocess by hand would have put a number in a report that nothing on disk could be held
  against; `dfu_s`, `update_s` and `attempts` in `flash.log` mean the next person to wonder
  whether 60 s is still twice an update can read it off the last flash. `attempts` is also the one
  field that says whether a scanner took one DFU or two, which is this task's own acceptance
  criterion.
- **`bench walk 9` gained a step, and `docs/bench.md` §9's watchdog bullet was rewritten.** The
  task's deliverable 5 is conditional (*if a command to provoke a hang is added*); it was, so the
  line is walked and ticked rather than left in *Not verified here*. The bullet now names `hang`
  and `hang 3000` and keeps what no session can do (the hardware watchdog) as the person's part.
- **Two writers of `tools/bench/walk.py` now pass `newline=""`.** `--tick` wrote
  `docs/bench.md` back through Python's text mode, which on Windows turns every `\n` into
  `\r\n`; this repository is LF in the working tree too, deliberately, *because CI compares hashes
  of files on disk* (`.gitattributes`). Git normalised the blob on commit, so nothing had reached
  `main` wrong, but the working tree a walk left behind differed from the repository and the next
  tool to read it would have seen `\r` at the end of every line. Found by the link loop, which
  parses `platformio.ini` with `awk` in a Linux container and could not find the repeater's band
  after a file of mine had been rewritten the same way (*Decisions*, 5). The same two-character
  fix is now in the page writer and the follow-ups writer.
- **The walk at `8ac514f` is the walk of this firmware, and one tool file changed after it.** That
  change is the `newline=""` above. It alters no byte the walk wrote except the line endings of
  the page it ticked, and not one line of `src/`, so the walk was not repeated for it; the result
  file names the commit the firmware is at.

## Decisions taken without asking

1. **`hang [ms]`, with a default of 12 000 ms and a clamp of 30 000.** The default has to outlast
   the 8 s deadline or `hang` with no argument would not demonstrate anything; the clamp has to
   stay well short of `WDT_TIMEOUT_S` or an argument could reach the *hardware* watchdog and leave
   a reader unable to say which of the two fired. A number that is not a number, and `0`, read as
   *no argument*. `Commands.HangDefaultsPastTheDeadlineAndIsClampedShortOfTheHardwareWatchdog` is
   those four sentences as a test.
2. **A `hang` shorter than the deadline answers an `err` and is not a failure.** It is the other
   half of the demonstration — *the check does not fire early* — and an `err` is the line a
   scanner has for *something did not go as the command said it would*.
3. **`hang` is not added to `console.py`'s refusals.** That guard exists for lines that take the
   node's identity, set a password, or hand the board to a bootloader this tool does not use.
   `hang` costs one reset of a role that never transmits and never writes flash, into the mode a
   watchdog reset boots into anyway; refusing it would have made §9's own line unwalkable by the
   one tool allowed to walk it.
4. **The loop check's cadence is 125 ms, so the deadline is reached between 8.000 s and 8.125 s.**
   The task says *if the loop has not come round in 8 s, reset* and the acceptance criterion says
   *within about 9 s*. A cadence that divides the deadline exactly makes the first number true to
   the millisecond, and `LoopWatch` is tested for never firing early at a cadence that does not
   divide it.
5. **The files I rewrote with a script were converted back to LF by hand** once the link loop
   found the first one. `platformio.ini`, the five scanner sources and `test_scanner.cpp` had been
   written through Python's text mode; nothing of their content changed, and `git diff` after the
   conversion is the content change alone.
6. **`loopWdtBegin()` leaves one heartbeat behind it**, so the deadline is counted from the start
   of the watch and not from the reset: `setup()` opens the ports, the radio and the I2C parts
   before `loop()` first runs. The exposure is the one the 8 s hardware watchdog already had
   (`wdtBegin()` is called in the same place), and the boot takes about 1.4 s.
7. **The linker fragment is not a SCons dependency.** Editing `loopwdt.ld` alone does not relink —
   found while proving the `ASSERT`s bite, which needed the `.elf` deleted first. It is written
   down here rather than fixed: a build that changes nothing else has nothing to relink for, and
   the file changes when the code that uses it changes.

## Not verified here (and why)

- **The hardware watchdog actually biting.** 60 s of a part whose RTC2 interrupt has also stopped
  is a state nothing on this bench can contrive: `hang` is clamped to 30 s precisely so that it
  cannot be mistaken for one, and a `hang` long enough would still be a loop the *interrupt* can
  see. What is shown here is that the register is written with the new `CRV` and that a 26.3 s DFU
  now fits inside it. `docs/bench.md` §9's watchdog bullet says this part is still nobody's.
- **`CONFIG.HALT = Pause` under a real debugger.** The Walter's SWD updates are what that word
  protects, and they need the Walter and the SWD lines
  (`twinoak.meshcore.walter-firmware`'s phase 6). The test is a grep; the proof is a J-Link.
- **A second RAK4630's update time.** 26.3 s is one chip, one adapter and one USB controller, four
  times. A unit whose DFU is materially slower would want the number re-derived — which is why the
  measurement is in `flash.log` on every flash and not only in this report, and why the comment at
  the define points at the method and not only at the result. The `p3.5-01` line of
  `docs/bench.md` §0 is where the next new unit is walked.
- **9.3, 9.10, 9.11 and 9.12 of §9**, unchanged from p3.5-02: `pwr` against the connector boards,
  the image check with a generator, the site through the cavity filter, and ten minutes of `scope`
  from the Walter UART with two consoles open.
- **The scanner's watchdog as a Walter sees it.** A Walter forwarding text to a scanner could now
  send `hang` and get a reset; `docs/noisescope.md` §5 says what that costs and why it is
  harmless, but the Walter's own half of that conversation is another repository's.
