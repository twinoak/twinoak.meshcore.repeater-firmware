# p3.5-04 — The clock and the thresholds, walked

**A clock has ticked on a RAK4630 now, and it does what the keeper was written to make it do.**
On the board, in one run: `clock.state` `unset` and upstream's own `clock` answering **15 May
2024**; a `time <epoch>` set moving the state to `set` within one pass of `loop()`; a `reboot`
coming back **`restored`, in the same minute as the set** — not 2024, not an hour on; and a
`clkreboot`, which sets upstream's clock back to 2024 on purpose, coming back `restored` and
**exactly 3600 s ahead** of where the set left it — never behind. `rtc` was never said, on a board
whose U5 is not fitted. `docs/bench.md` §3 and §5 are walks now (`bench walk 3`, `bench walk 5`),
and seven of their lines are ticked *bench*.

**One line of the page does not hold, and it is a page line and not a board fault.** `clock sync`
at the USB console answers `ERR: clock cannot go backwards` and can never do anything else: it
sets the clock to the **sender's** timestamp plus one (`CommonCLI.cpp:199`–`:207` at the pin
`d9296435`), and our console calls `handleCommand(0, …)` (`src/roles/repeater/main.cpp:176`), so
there is no sender. Over the mesh, where an admin's message carries a timestamp, the page's line
is right. That is a *differs*, written into `tasks/phase-1/FOLLOWUPS.md` as `[04]` — p1-04 wrote
the line — and not fixed here.

**The thresholds are settable, survive a restart, refuse what they should, and were put back.**
`power.low_mv` 3600 → `ok`, on `sensor list 5`, still 3600 after a `reboot`; `3350`, `2400` and
`4400` each `can't find custom var` with the value unchanged after each; and the walk's last step
is both thresholds back at the 3500/3300 it found.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **§3's and §5's mixed bullets were split, which the task's deliverable 5 asks for** (*their
  lines say what the walk does and what stays yours*) and which p3.3-03 deliberately did not do
  for §1. The page's own claims were kept word for word; what moved is where a bullet's boundary
  is, so that a line is either a console line (a walk's) or a link/meter/switch line (a person's)
  and never half of each:
  - §3's single *On the USB console…* bullet became **six**: the node that has never had a clock,
    the `time <epoch>` set, `clock sync`, the `reboot`, the `clkreboot`, and `rtc` never said on
    a board with no RV-3028. The `clkreboot` and *no RTC fitted* lines are the task's own
    deliverables and the page had neither as a line of its own (`clkreboot` was named inside the
    companion bullet, which stays a person's).
  - §5's *The thresholds are settable and survive a restart* bullet became **two**: the set that
    sticks, and the pair this firmware will not take. One line for a thing that holds and another
    for a thing that is refused, so that a board that does one and not the other ticks one box.
  - Both keep **`p1-04`** / **`p1-06`** as their task, because a *differs* belongs to the task
    that wrote the behaviour (the task says so), and that is also what sent 3.9 to phase 1's
    follow-ups rather than to this phase's.
  - The page's `cli "…"` wording for the thresholds is now *at the USB console — `cli "…"` over
    the link is the same interpreter, and `reboot 0` is the link's word for the console's
    `reboot`*: the walk sends the console's half, and the sentence says the link's is the same
    interpreter rather than silently claiming the link was walked.
- **The repeater image is not byte-reproducible, and that is why this report does not claim the
  board carried byte-identical bytes before the flash.** Asking "is the running image this
  commit's?" turned up that two builds of the **same** source differ: a clean rebuild of

  `RADIOLIB_VALUE(__DATE__) " " RADIOLIB_VALUE(__TIME__)` (`src/BuildOpt.h:656`) and whose string
  is in the linked image. `out/bench/flash.log`

  repeater image changed. Nothing of this task depends on it, so it is a `[reviewer]` follow-up
  in this phase's `FOLLOWUPS.md` and not a fix: it is the release's question (a manifest's

  **`bench flash` wrote this commit's own build to the board before the walk** (22:09:31,

## Decisions taken without asking

1. **The walk does not flash; the task's *fresh flash* was `bench flash` before it.** §3 and §5
   read consoles and reset the board with upstream's own `reboot`/`clkreboot`; neither opens a
   run. The precondition of §3's first line — a node whose clock has never been set — was made by
   flashing this commit's repeater package in a run of its own, and that run was closed with
   `bench restore` after both walks. A flash does **not** itself make a clock `unset` (the
   keeper's `/clock` survives it), which is decision 2.
2. **A node this section has already been walked on is *skipped*, not a *differs*.** `clock.state`
   `unset` and the 2024 date can only be read on a node whose clock has never been set, and only
   an `erase` (`bench walk 10`'s, not this walk's) takes a node back there. A second `bench walk 3`
   says so in those words and walks on; and because a clock this firmware keeps only goes forward,
   the `time`, `clock sync` and `reboot` lines are skipped too when the node's clock is already
   ahead of the walker's — with the number of seconds it is ahead in the reason. That is p3.5-01's
   rule (*already ticked*, not *not found*) applied to a line rather than to a tick.
3. **The clock is the one thing a walk cannot put back, and the page and the result file say so.**
   The node is left `restored` and about an hour ahead of the real time — the `clkreboot` line's
   own margin, `kSaveIntervalSeconds`. The keeper's memories are a high-water mark on purpose
   (`ClockKeeper.h`), the Walter corrects the time within seconds of `EV_BOOT`
   (`spec/LINK-PROTOCOL.md` §5.4), and the alternative — an `erase` — would take the identity the
   acceptance criterion requires unchanged.
4. **§5's three refused values are refusals of a node whose `power.crit_mv` is 3300**, which is
   what the page's line assumes and what a node nobody has touched holds (`kDefaultCritMv`).
   `3350` and `2400` are judged against `crit`, so on a node holding another one some of the three
   are pairs this firmware accepts: the walk then *skips* that line naming the `crit` it found,
   rather than calling a correct `ok` wrong. The walk does not reimplement
   `thresholdsAcceptable`; it reads `crit` and compares it to the one number the page's line
   rests on.
5. **The put-back is a step of 5.5 and the walk's last step**, and it tries both orders (`low`
   then `crit`, then the other way) because a pair is accepted or refused as a pair. A walk that
   could not put them back would say so in capitals, with the two lines to type by hand; this one
   did not have to.
6. **§5's paging line is *skipped* because `bench walk 1` reads the same two pages at the same
   console** (its 1.1 holds `10 vars`, the five keys of each page and the cut digit), and the
   page's own wording for it is the link's `cli`. That is §1.7's precedent, which is skipped
   because `bench walk 8` is what it describes.
7. **`clock sync`'s expectation is the page's claim, and the walk judges it on the board.** It
   would have been easy to write the walk around what the pin's source says it must answer at a
   port with no sender; then the page would still say something untrue and nobody would be told.
   The step sends the line, holds it to the page, and when it differs the note names the two
   lines of source that explain it and says it is p1-04's to fix.
8. **The tick tag names this branch's code commit, `b391890`.** `tick_page` writes
   `(bench, <date>, <commit>)` from `git rev-parse HEAD`, which during the walk was still
   `main`'s `1904723` — the branch had no commit yet, and §3 cannot be walked a second time on a
   clock that has moved (decision 2), so the walk could not simply be repeated after the commit
   as p3.5-02 and p3.5-03 did. `b391890` is the code the walk ran, so the seven tags and the
   follow-up item were set to it in this commit; the result files under `out/bench/` are named
   and headed `1904723`, the commit the tool could see, and they are the evidence either way.

## Not verified here (and why)

The board was on this machine and both walks ran on it. What is left is what no USB console can
reach, with what each line needs — the same text is in `docs/bench.md` §3 and §5, and in the two
result files under *Still a person's*.

**The clock (§3), seven lines:**

- **3.1, 3.2** — `linkcli.py time`, `time set` and `reboot 0`, and `status`'s flag bit 3: the box
  link, which needs the 3.3 V dongle on the link pins (`bench.local.json`'s `dongle_serial`, not
  on this bench) or the Walter. The console's half of 3.2 is walked (3.8 and 3.10).
- **3.3** — the **reset switch**, and a watchdog reset on a repeater image: a hand on the board,
  and a way to provoke one. Both leave the firmware no chance to write its file, so the clock
  comes back up to an hour ahead rather than in the same second — the case 3.10 does *not* show.
- **3.4** — **power off for a minute**, and `reset_reason 1 (power-on)` after it: the lab supply's
  switch and the link.
- **3.5, 3.6** — a **companion's view of the adverts**: the hold (an advert about a minute after
  boot, at once when the clock is set within a second of `EV_BOOT`), and a companion that still
  accepts this node's adverts after a power cycle. A second radio in range and a phone; a session
  cannot hear a radio.
- **3.13** — an **RV-3028 hand-fitted** on `0x52`: `time_state 3 (rtc)` after a power cycle, no
  advert hold, and a set written through to the part. U5 is DNP on this batch and nobody on this
  bench has the part.

**And 3.9 is a *differs*, not a person's:** `clock sync` at the USB console cannot move a clock
(`sender_timestamp` is 0). `tasks/phase-1/FOLLOWUPS.md` has it as `[04]`; what a bench could still
show is the mesh half — `clock sync` in an admin's message from a phone, which does carry a
timestamp.

**Power (§5), six lines** — every one of them needs the production connector boards, a meter, a
phone, the lab supply's knob or a hand on the I2C bus:

- **5.1 [boards]** — `linkcli.py power` against a **DMM** in series with the panel lead: sign
  first, then size, on all three channels.
- **5.2 [boards]** — the same three numbers in a **phone's telemetry** view (LPP channels 2, 3, 4)
  and the battery voltage in its `status`. No phone has ever read this node's telemetry.
- **5.3 [boards]** — `linkcli.py env` against a **thermometer**, and the humidity and pressure
  against something trustworthy.
- **5.4** — the **thresholds doing their job**: the pack stepped below 3500 mV and held a minute →
  `EV_ATTENTION` reason 2; below 3300 mV → reason 3; a dip of a few seconds raising nothing; the
  100 mV of hysteresis and the hourly limit. The lab supply's knob (or a simulator) and the link.
  This walk showed the two thresholds are **settable and kept**; what happens when the voltage
  crosses one is native-tested (`test/test_power`) and still unwalked.
- **5.7 [boards]** — the **INA3221's bus unplugged** (or SDA held low, the reviewer's option) and
  plugged back in.
- **5.9** — the **boot-voltage question, the reviewer's**: a node coming up with the pack at
  3.2 V. Its three `get`s are read above and filled into the page's blank
  (`0 mV` / `supported` / `Reset: Soft Reset; Shutdown: Unknown`); the knob is a person's.

**And one thing for the reviewer, found on the way** (this phase's `FOLLOWUPS.md`): a release's
bytes are not reproducible from its source — RadioLib's `__DATE__ " " __TIME__` sits in every
image, so two builds of one commit differ in three bytes and a manifest's SHA-256 is per-build.
